Good morning, hockey fans. It's Saturday, October thirty-first, Halloween, and here's your rundown of Friday night in the NHL. It was a quiet one, just three games, but we had a first career hat trick in Florida, a Devils team getting hot with Connor Hellebuyck in net, and a Calgary blowout that left the Seattle coach calling it the worst forty minutes of his tenure. Plus a big trade request out of Tampa. Let's go game by game.

We start in Sunrise, where the Florida Panthers beat the Winnipeg Jets five to two. Anton Lundell had his first NHL hat trick, in his three hundred seventy-second game, after coming in with just one goal in thirteen games. The game winner was Seth Jones, whose power play goal in the second period made it three to one, and he also had two assists, giving him eighteen points in October, a Florida record for a defenseman in a month. Sam Reinhart had a goal and an assist, and Akira Schmid made twenty-two saves for his third straight win. Kyle Connor scored on the power play and Morgan Barron got his first goal of the season for Winnipeg, and Nico Daws made thirty-three saves in his first start since coming over in the Hellebuyck trade, but the Jets have now lost eight straight and are zero, six and two in their last eight. Barron said there are no moral victories right now.

In Las Vegas, the New Jersey Devils beat the Golden Knights five to two. The game winner was Jack Hughes, who skated through three defenders for a go ahead goal early in the second period, and he added an empty netter for two on the night. Arseny Gritsyuk also scored twice, including the tying goal late in the first, and Luke Hughes opened the scoring. Hellebuyck made nineteen saves for his second straight win since the October twenty-third trade from Winnipeg, and the Devils have won five of their last six. Mark Stone and Ivan Barbashev scored for Vegas, and Mitch Marner had two assists, but Adin Hill made twenty-seven saves in the loss, and Vegas has gone one, three and one in its last five, failing to hold leads. Barbashev said, quote, just didn't play well.

And in Calgary, the Flames beat the Kraken six to two. Matt Coronato had a goal and three assists for his first four point game, and the game winner was Matvei Gridin, whose power play goal in the second period made it three to one. Joel Farabee had a goal and two assists, Morgan Frost had a goal and an assist, and Aydar Suniev and Ryan Strome also scored, with Dustin Wolf making twenty saves. Philipp Grubauer was pulled in the second period after stopping ten of fourteen shots, with Joey Daccord coming in and making eleven saves. Frederick Gaudreau and Mackie Samoskevich scored for Seattle, but coach Lane Lambert called the final two periods probably the worst forty minutes of his tenure, citing turnovers, defensive mistakes and the power play goals allowed.

Now a few league headlines. The big one is in Tampa, and the key point is that this part is reported, not settled. Elliotte Friedman of Sportsnet is reporting that Nikita Kucherov, the reigning MVP, has told teammates and the organization that he will not re-sign at the end of this season and has asked for a trade. That is a report. What is confirmed is the response from the Lightning. General manager Julien BriseBois put out a statement saying the team unequivocally has no intention of trading Nikita. Kucherov is in the final year of his contract and, per Pierre LeBrun, has a list of ten approved destinations rather than a full no trade clause. Elsewhere, the Red Wings are reportedly in no rush to move Dylan Larkin, saying they will wait as long as necessary to find the right deal.

On discipline, confirmed, the NHL Department of Player Safety suspended Ottawa's Hayden Hodgson two games for the elbow to the head of Kings defenseman Brandt Clarke. On injuries, Colorado defenseman Josh Manson is out about eight weeks with an ankle injury, which would put his return around Christmas, and Calgary's Devin Cooley is week to week. Florida's Aleksander Barkov has started skating and could return in about four weeks. Ottawa placed Warren Foegele on injured reserve with a knee injury, and Vancouver put Filip Chytil on injured reserve with an upper body injury, with a return possible next Thursday. Pro Hockey Rumors also lists Seattle's Jared McCann as expected to miss ten weeks, Pittsburgh's Bryan Rust out six weeks, and Montreal's Nick Suzuki out two to four weeks. In roster moves, the Red Wings recalled defenseman Jacob Bernard-Docker, and the Blues recalled Theo Lindstein, with Colton Parayko listed as probable for Saturday. And sadly, Matt Pavelich, the first linesman in the Hall of Fame, has died at ninety-two. The trade deadline is Monday, March first, which is one hundred twenty-one days away, a little over four months.

Now the Blackhawks, who did not play last night. The Blackhawks last game was a four to one road loss against the Detroit Red Wings on Thursday night. They play today, Saturday, at Boston, puck drop at one p.m. Eastern, noon Central, at TD Garden, the finale of their current road trip, before they come home to host Colorado on Monday.

On what the Blackhawks blogs are saying, the common thread is that the offense has gone quiet and the pressure is building on coach Jeff Blashill. Chicago Hockey Now ran a piece headlined too quiet behind Bedard, Second City Hockey wrote that Blashill could be in danger if results do not change quickly, and Blackhawk Up headlined the Detroit game an embarrassing outing and asked whether big changes are ahead. The bright spot is Roman Kantserov, who leads the team with five goals in thirteen games, though Second City Hockey notes his shooting percentage is likely to come down. The team is four, nine and zero, and the blogs point to a rough stretch, with four of the next five games on the road against strong opponents. I could not get the figures for power play and penalty kill from the NHL dot com team stats page this morning, so I will skip the special teams numbers today rather than quote another site.

On roster news, the last moves I could verify are Andrew Mangiapane activated from injured reserve on October twenty-third and Dominic Toninato assigned to Rockford on October eighteenth. As for rumors, and these are only rumors, Chicago Hockey Now lists headlines saying the Blackhawks are showing interest in a Capitals winger and that Kevin Korchinski is on the trade board, but I could only see the headlines, so treat that as unconfirmed.

On tonight's opponent, the Bruins are nine, four and one, with nineteen points, first in the Atlantic by points, though Tampa Bay has a better points percentage with seventeen points in twelve games, and Montreal has eighteen in thirteen. Boston has lost two straight, including that four to one loss in St. Louis. One preview lists Spencer Knight against Jeremy Swayman in goal, but I could not confirm either starter or Boston's last ten record or hot players from a source this morning. After Boston, Chicago hosts Colorado on Monday, then goes to Florida on Wednesday, Tampa Bay on Thursday and Carolina next Saturday.

Now the league leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with fourteen, Leon Draisaitl of Edmonton has twelve, Kyle Connor of Winnipeg has eleven, and three players are tied with ten, Auston Matthews, Artturi Lehkonen and Alex Tuch. In assists, Connor McDavid is way out front with twenty-eight, then four players are tied at sixteen, Matthew Tkachuk, Quinn Hughes, Seth Jones and Adam Fox, and Evan Bouchard has fifteen. In points, McDavid has thirty-six, Kaprizov and Draisaitl are tied at twenty-four, Matthews has twenty-three, and Nathan MacKinnon and Bouchard are tied at twenty-two.

Finally, the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty-four points, and the Hurricanes are first overall in the league by both points and points percentage. In the Central, Minnesota leads with twenty-one points. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with nineteen points in fourteen games, though Tampa Bay has a better points percentage with seventeen points in twelve games, and Montreal has eighteen in thirteen. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with twenty points in thirteen games, ahead of Vegas with nineteen in fifteen. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with seven points, and Chicago is next to last with eight. Then there is a three way tie for third from the bottom at ten points, Columbus, the Kings and Philadelphia, and Philadelphia has the worst points percentage of the group because it has played fifteen games.

That's your rundown. Have a great Saturday, and we'll see you tomorrow.
