Good morning, hockey fans. It's Thursday, October twenty second, and here's your NHL rundown. It was a quiet Wednesday night, just two games on the schedule, so we'll have time to get into the league news, the Blackhawks, the leaderboards and the standings.

We start in Buffalo, where the Philadelphia Flyers beat the Sabres three to one in regulation. Rookie Cole Knuble stole the show with the first two goals of his NHL career, one late in the first period and another early in the third. He is the son of former Flyers forward Mike Knuble, and he is the first Flyers rookie to score twice in one of his first two games since Vinny Prospal back in nineteen ninety six. Trevor Zegras added a power play goal with less than a minute left in the first, and that one goes down as the game winner under the usual convention. Oliver Bonk had two assists, and goalie Dan Vladar turned aside twenty seven shots. Jason Zucker scored Buffalo's only goal on the power play in his season debut after offseason surgery, but Ukko-Pekka Luukkonen could not get much help, and coach Lindy Ruff said his team lost the battle around the net. Philadelphia has now won three straight.

Next, Ottawa, where the Senators crushed the Florida Panthers five to one. The big storyline was Brady Tkachuk's return to the building where he spent eight seasons. He got a standing ovation, then scored on the power play in the first period for Florida. After that, it was all Ottawa. Tim Stutzle tied it on the power play, and Claude Giroux put the Sens ahead for good in the second period, the game winner. Shane Pinto made it three to one late in the second, and then Warren Foegele and Stephen Halliday each scored short handed goals in the third. Jake Sanderson came back from an eight game absence and had two assists. Akira Schmid made twenty five saves for Florida but got little support, and coach Paul Maurice said his team was behind the play in the first period, lost too many faceoffs and spent too much time in the box. The one sour note for Ottawa is goalie Linus Ullmark, who left early in the third period with a lower body strain. He'll be reassessed today. Florida's three game winning streak is over.

Now the wider league. A few rumors first, and these are rumors, not confirmed. The big one is Connor Hellebuyck. The Winnipeg goalie has been suspended for not reporting to camp, and that suspension ends Friday. Pro Hockey Rumors, citing Elliotte Friedman of Sportsnet, says it ends October twenty third. Pro Hockey Rumors also reports that a deal with Buffalo was close but fell through, and ESPN's Emily Kaplan is reporting that three teams remain in the mix. If he isn't traded by Friday, Winnipeg will keep paying him but try to keep him away from the team. Another rumor site links the Rangers to goalies after word that Igor Shesterkin is out for the season, so keep an eye on that. And The Hockey Writers rumor roundup says the Red Wings are in advanced talks on a Dylan Larkin deal. That is a rumor from one outlet, so take it with salt.

Confirmed injury news: Panthers goalie Jacob Markstrom is on injured reserve for ten to fourteen days. Stars defenseman Esa Lindell is out at least a week. Oilers forward Ryan Nugent-Hopkins is set to return tonight. Buffalo's Rasmus Dahlin returned after missing three games, and the Hurricanes may get Seth Jarvis back soon. Toronto's Jake McCabe left a game against Columbus and the team is waiting on his status.

Now the Chicago Blackhawks. They did not play last night. Their last game was Saturday, a four to nothing loss to the Dallas Stars at the United Center. Jake Oettinger made nineteen saves for his third shutout of the season, Arttu Hyry scored twice, and Spencer Knight stopped twenty nine shots for Chicago in his fourth straight start. It was the first loss for the Hawks with Connor Bedard in the lineup this season. Bedard said the team just wasn't crisp, and that the five man unit failed to get the puck out of its own zone. Coach Jeff Blashill said the team is creating much more offense than at the start of the year but is still giving up too much. The Hawks did hold a slight edge in five on five chances, but they struggled on the penalty kill and at four on four. Earlier in the week, Artyom Levshunov scored in overtime to beat Winnipeg four to three, and Patrick Kane set a record for assists by a United States born player on a Bedard goal.

On the roster front, defenseman Bowen Byram is day to day with an upper body injury and has missed two games, though Blashill says he is not in concussion protocol. Forward Andrew Mangiapane is on injured reserve but has been skating in a no contact jersey, and ESPN lists an estimated return of October twenty fifth. Dominic Toninato was sent down to Rockford on Sunday, and Eddie Olczyk was named a team ambassador. The Hawks are three and six, last in the Central Division, with six points.

Next up is the Montreal Canadiens on Friday night at the United Center. Montreal is four, three and two, ten points, sixth in the Atlantic Division, and coming off an overtime loss. Their last ten games are the same as their season record, four wins, three regulation losses and two overtime losses. I couldn't find current reporting on their hot players, so check the box scores. Then the Panthers visit Sunday.

On to the leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with eleven. Behind him there's a big tie at eight goals between Auston Matthews, Leon Draisaitl, Mark Stone, Kyle Connor and Artturi Lehkonen. In assists, Connor McDavid leads with twenty, then Jack Eichel with thirteen, Matthew Tkachuk with twelve, and Evan Bouchard and Quinn Hughes tied with eleven. In points, McDavid leads with twenty six. Matthews, Bouchard and Eichel are tied for second with eighteen, and Kaprizov has seventeen.

Finally, the standings. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with fifteen points, though Tampa Bay has fourteen in one fewer game. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with seventeen points. In the Central, Utah and Minnesota are tied with fourteen points each. In the Pacific, Vegas leads with sixteen. The top team in the league is Carolina with seventeen points, followed by Vegas with sixteen. Edmonton has fifteen points and the best points percentage in the league because they've played only nine games. At the bottom, Calgary and Los Angeles are tied for last with four points, though Los Angeles has played only seven games. Vancouver is next with five points. The Blackhawks and Columbus are just above them at six.

That's your rundown. Enjoy the day, and we'll see you tomorrow.
