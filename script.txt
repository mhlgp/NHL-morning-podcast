Good morning, hockey fans. It's Friday, October thirtieth, and here's your rundown of Thursday night in the NHL. It was a full slate of eleven games, with three that went to overtime, a wild seven six finish in Anaheim, a hat trick for Logan Cooley that saved a point at the last second, and a Blackhawks loss in a game overshadowed by drama in Detroit. Let's go game by game.

We start in Detroit, where the Red Wings beat the Blackhawks four to one. Viktor Arvidsson had a hat trick, including an empty netter, and the game winner belonged to Lucas Raymond, whose power play goal early in the third period made it two to one. Connor Bedard gave Chicago the lead in the first period with a deflection of a Wyatt Kaiser shot, but the Hawks managed only eleven shots over the last two periods, and Spencer Knight made twenty-seven saves in a losing effort. John Gibson stopped eighteen for Detroit, and Alex DeBrincat had two assists against his old team. I'll come back to the Hawks, and to the Dylan Larkin situation, in a bit.

In Tampa, the Lightning beat the Jets six to four. The game winner was Brandon Hagel's goal late in the second period, which made it five to one, and he finished with a goal and two assists. Pontus Holmberg set a career high with four points, including the empty netter, Anthony Cirelli had a goal and two assists, and Andrei Vasilevskiy made seventeen saves. Neal Pionk scored twice for Winnipeg and played his six hundredth game, and Kyle Connor made it five to four late, but the Jets have now lost seven straight. Stuart Skinner allowed five goals on twenty-one shots and was replaced by Nico Daws to start the third.

In Pittsburgh, the Utah Mammoth beat the Penguins four to three in overtime, and Mikhail Sergachev scored the winner three minutes and twenty-seven seconds into the extra period. Logan Cooley had a hat trick, and the third one was a dandy, because he tied it with just one second left in regulation after Pittsburgh had gone ahead on a Ben Kindel goal with twenty-five seconds to play. Rickard Rakell had scored shorthanded to tie it earlier in the third, and Erik Karlsson also scored for Pittsburgh. Sebastian Cossa made thirty-one saves for Utah, and Arturs Silovs made thirty for the Penguins, who let a point slip away that late.

In Raleigh, the Hurricanes beat the Blue Jackets three to two. Mark Jankowski scored sixteen seconds into the third period for the game winner, set up by a Seth Jarvis steal, and Jordan Martinook and Logan Stankoven also scored. Pyotr Kochetkov made twenty-four saves, which pushed his season opening win streak to nine games, a Carolina franchise record. Columbus got goals from Denton Mateychuk and a power play goal from Kent Johnson and outplayed Carolina for the last two periods, but a poor first period cost them, and Jet Greaves made twenty-four saves in defeat.

In St. Louis, the Blues beat the Bruins four to one. Philip Broberg scored the game winner early in the second period, and he and Robert Thomas each had a goal and two assists. Jimmy Snuggerud and Jake Neighbours each scored on the power play, and Joel Hofer made thirty-one saves, fourteen of them in the third. James Hagens gave Boston the early lead, but Bruins coach Marco Sturm called the first forty minutes pretty bad, and Boston has lost two straight after an eight game point streak.

In Nashville, the Capitals beat the Predators four to three. Alex Tuch scored the game winner with a wrist shot at ten minutes and nine seconds of the third period, his tenth goal of the season and his fifth game winner, which leads the league. Cole Hutson had the first two goal game of his NHL career, and Boone Jenner also scored, with Charlie Lindgren making twenty-four saves. Filip Forsberg scored twice for Nashville, the first on a five on three power play, and Roman Josi had three assists, but the Predators gave up some uncharacteristic goals during lulls.

In Dallas, the Canadiens beat the Stars five to two. Cole Caufield and Alex Newhook each scored twice, and Caufield's second goal, just twenty-seven seconds after Newhook's first, made it three to one and stood as the game winner. Phillip Danault added an empty netter for his first goal of the season, and Jakub Dobes made seventeen saves. That's four straight wins for Montreal, all on the road. Wyatt Johnston scored on the power play for Dallas, and Jamie Benn also scored, but Stars coach Glen Gulutzan said the special teams success didn't make up for four goals allowed at five on five.

In Minnesota, the Islanders beat the Wild four to three in overtime, and rookie defenseman Matthew Schaefer was the hero with two goals, the second coming thirty-three seconds into overtime on a two on one. That made him the first teenage defenseman with three career overtime goals. Bo Horvat scored on the power play and Adam Pelech also scored, and Ilya Sorokin made twenty-two saves. Quinn Hughes had a goal and an assist for Minnesota, and Joel Eriksson Ek and Matt Boldy scored too, but Boldy's goal came off a Schaefer turnover and the Wild lost their first home game of the season. The Islanders did lose defenseman Ryan Pulock early with an upper body injury, and there's no update yet.

In Anaheim, the Sabres beat the Ducks seven to six in a wild one. Zach Benson scored the game winner, Buffalo's seventh goal, and Tage Thompson had a natural hat trick, three goals in a span of about three minutes, plus two assists. Buffalo trailed three to one early and then scored six straight goals, with Peyton Krebs adding three assists and Colten Ellis making twenty-nine saves. Anaheim got two power play goals from Cutter Gauthier, a goal and an assist from Beckett Sennecke, and the first NHL goal for Roger McQueen, plus three assists from Leo Carlsson, but Ville Husso allowed seven goals on thirty-one shots, and coach Joel Quenneville said the puck is going in too easily. The Ducks have lost seven of their last eight.

In Los Angeles, the Kings beat the Senators six to five in overtime, and Quinton Byfield scored the winner. The Kings got four goals in the second period, from Trevor Moore, Mikey Anderson, Joel Armia and Artemi Panarin, and led five to one, but Ottawa came all the way back. Dylan Cozens had two goals and an assist, Drake Batherson had a goal and two assists, and Shane Pinto tied it with one minute and forty seconds left in regulation. Drew Doughty had three assists for Los Angeles, and Jonas Johansson was pulled in the second period for Ottawa, with Samuel Ersson stopping twenty in relief. Defenseman Brandt Clarke did not return for the Kings after an elbow to the head from Hayden Hodgson, who got a major penalty and a game misconduct.

And in San Jose, the Sharks beat the Canucks three to two with three goals in two minutes and fifty seconds in the third period. Will Smith scored twice, and Mason Marchment scored eleven seconds after Smith's tying goal for the game winner. Yaroslav Askarov made twenty-three saves, and forward Filip Bystedt made his NHL debut. Vancouver got goals from Drew O'Connor and Liam Ohgren and led two nothing, but the Canucks are one, ten and one in their last twelve, and coach Manny Malhotra said face off details once again plagued them.

Now a few league headlines. The big one is in Detroit, and this part is confirmed. The Red Wings suspended Dylan Larkin without pay on Thursday, and interim general manager Shawn Horcoff said Larkin has informed the team that he will no longer play for Detroit. Any trade needs Larkin's approval and has to serve the team's long term interests. Larkin asked for a trade over the summer and was stripped of the captaincy in September. As for what happens next, those are reports, not deals. ESPN reports trade talks are ongoing, and Pierre LeBrun says talks have escalated but a deal is not necessarily imminent, and that the Wild have made the most offers to Detroit. Sports Illustrated reports the Devils, Capitals, Wild and Hurricanes have all engaged in talks. Pierre LeBrun also reports the Sharks have had talks with Detroit about restricted free agent defenseman Simon Edvinsson, and Detroit is still hoping to re-sign him.

In other rumor news, and again these are only reports, Elliotte Friedman said on the thirty two Thoughts podcast that Minnesota could consider moving Quinn Hughes if they believe it would better position them to build a championship caliber roster. The Hockey Writers lists Maple Leafs general manager John Chayka as not ruling out trading Easton Cowan. And Pierre LeBrun says Calgary general manager Craig Conroy would like to talk to forward Morgan Frost about a contract extension. The trade deadline is Monday, March first, which is one hundred and twenty-two days away, a little over four months. On injuries, Calgary's Devin Cooley and Brennan Othmann, and Colorado's Josh Manson, were hurt earlier this week, and Thursday added Pulock for the Islanders and Brandt Clarke for the Kings. I did not find any new league suspensions beyond Larkin.

Now the Blackhawks, who lost to Detroit four to one on the road on Thursday night. Chicago fell to four, nine and zero, has lost four of its last five, and is zero and five on the road. Bedard opened the scoring at five fifty nine of the first with a deflection, his third goal of the season, which extended his point streak to four games, and Roman Kantserov picked up his first NHL assist. The top line of Teuvo Teravainen, Bedard and Kantserov controlled the first period, and Kantserov hit a post. After that, the Hawks only had eleven shots over the final two periods. Arvidsson tied it in the second, and Frank Nazar was stopped on a penalty shot after Andreas Englund hooked him on a breakaway, a chance that would have put Chicago back in front. Then Patrick Kane went to the box for high sticking early in the third, and Raymond scored on the power play to give Detroit the lead for good. Kane also got a standing ovation and a video tribute in his first return to Detroit.

Coach Jeff Blashill did not hold back. He called it a major step backward and criticized the team's skating, competing and attention to detail, and said they have had a lot of games where they did good things but beat themselves with critical mistakes. Chicago Hockey Now called the offense sterile, noting the team has scored just six goals in its last five games and has not reached twenty-two shots in five of its last seven. The Big Lead and Yahoo focused on Blashill's blunt criticism, and Blackhawk Up headlined it an embarrassing outing and asked whether big changes are ahead. The consensus is that the effort and the offense were the problem, and that Spencer Knight gave them a chance and can't be blamed.
