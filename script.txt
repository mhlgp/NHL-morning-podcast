Good morning, hockey fans. It's Tuesday, October twenty-seventh, and here is your rundown of Monday night in the NHL. Three games on the board, so let's get right into it.

We start in New York, where the Los Angeles Kings beat the Rangers six to three at Madison Square Garden. The Kings jumped on them early. Taylor Ward scored on a breakaway off a Drew Doughty pass just one minute and fifteen seconds in, and Brandt Clarke made it two on a power play before the five minute mark. J.T. Miller got one back for New York, but Doughty answered with a one-timer from the point for his first goal of the year, and he finished with three points, his first three-point night since February of last year. Artemi Panarin, playing against his old team, scored a power-play goal early in the second to make it four to one, and that was the game winner. Mikey Anderson added another, Adam Fox and Tye Kartye scored for the Rangers, and Joel Edmundson sealed it into the empty net. Darcy Kuemper made twenty-three saves for Los Angeles. It was a rough night for James Reimer, who was making his first start for the Rangers after signing a one-year deal on Sunday. He gave up three goals on eight shots and got pulled in the first period, with Dylan Garand finishing up in relief. The Rangers have lost three straight, and Miller said they simply weren't playing their style.

Next, up in Calgary, the Flames beat the Maple Leafs five to two for their fourth straight win. Rookie Matvei Gridin was the star, with two goals and an assist, the first three-point game of his NHL career. His second goal, early in the second period on the power play, made it three to nothing and stood up as the game winner. Mikael Backlund scored in his return from injury, and Joel Farabee and Martin Pospisil added empty-netters. Devin Cooley stopped twenty-six shots for his fourth straight start and win. Toronto got goals from Easton Cowan and William Nylander, and Auston Matthews picked up an assist on Nylander's goal for the eight hundredth point of his career, the fastest anyone has gotten there in Maple Leafs history. But coach Jim Hiller said his team turned the puck over way too much, and Sergei Bobrovsky took the loss in his eight hundredth NHL start despite twenty-nine saves. Toronto also has to watch Kirill Marchenko, who left in the second period with a lower-body injury.

The late game was in Edmonton, where the Detroit Red Wings beat the Oilers four to three in a shootout. Rookie goalie Michal Postava was the story, making twenty-five saves in his first NHL start and stopping all three shootout attempts for his first career win, and Lucas Raymond scored the only shootout goal for the winner. Raymond also had two assists in regulation. Vasily Podkolzin, Isaac Howard and Colton Dach scored for Edmonton, while Viktor Arvidsson and Emmitt Finnie scored for Detroit, and then Dylan Larkin tied it late with the extra attacker. Connor McDavid assisted on a goal to stretch his season-opening point streak to twelve games, and his assist streak ties the NHL record for a start to a season. The Oilers did lose Zach Hyman in the second period after a hit sent his head into the glass, and Edmonton has dropped two straight. Tristan Jarry made twenty-six saves in the loss.

Now for the league headlines. First, confirmed news. The Winnipeg Jets traded goalie Connor Hellebuyck to the New Jersey Devils on Friday, bringing the long saga to an end. Winnipeg got Dawson Mercer, Nick Bjugstad, Nico Daws, prospect Alexander Command and draft picks. On the injury front, Pro Hockey Rumors reports that the Kings placed Mats Zuccarello on injured reserve with an upper-body injury, and that Quinton Byfield may be out as well. The Flyers will be without Rasmus Ristolainen for about two weeks. Canadiens center Nick Suzuki is out two to four weeks, Penguins forward Bryan Rust is out six weeks, and Kraken forward Jared McCann is expected to miss about ten weeks. Senators defenseman Thomas Chabot is nearing a return after a full practice Monday. On the transaction wire, the Rangers signed James Reimer to that one-year deal, and the Hurricanes re-signed Alexander Nikishin to a one-year contract. On the discipline side, I found no new suspensions from Monday night.

Now the rumors, and these are rumors, not done deals. Sportsnet's Elliotte Friedman says the Capitals have given forward Ivan Miroshnichenko permission to seek a trade, though Washington wants a legitimate return. Friedman has also reported that the Maple Leafs have told other teams they want a premium asset in exchange for a difference-making defenseman. Pro Hockey Rumors is reporting that the Blue Jackets are listening to offers for Dmitri Voronkov.

Now to our Chicago Blackhawks. They did not play Monday night. Their last game was Sunday afternoon at the United Center, a three to two loss to the Florida Panthers, their third straight defeat. They fell to three and eight. Ryan Greene scored in the first period, his first goal of the season, and Roman Kantserov tied it in the second. Lars Eller won it for Florida with two minutes and twenty-three seconds left, after a bad Chicago line change, and the Hawks went oh for seven on the power play. Spencer Knight made thirty saves. Defenseman Kevin Korchinski, back after sitting seven straight games, was arguably the team's best defenseman with an assist, but he was the one on the ice for that costly late mistake, according to Chicago Hockey Now. Ryan Donato's stick broke on a great look that could have made it three to two, and Frank Nazar was stopped on a shorthanded breakaway. Coach Jeff Blashill said the power play is mirroring the team's five-on-five struggles, and captain Connor Bedard said the frustration is understandable and should be used to get better. There was a nice moment before the game as the team honored Ian Cole for his one thousandth NHL game. Patrick Kane also sits at eight hundred ninety-eight career assists, two shy of becoming the first American skater to reach nine hundred.

On the roster front, defenseman Bowen Byram is day-to-day with an upper-body injury and has missed four straight. Andrew Mangiapane was expected back in the lineup, and Ryan Ellis is on injured reserve and out for the season. Down in Rockford, the IceHogs beat Toronto three to nothing behind a shutout from Drew Commesso, and Nick Lardis has six points in five games since being sent down. Prospect Ryan Roobroeck had a hat trick, including the overtime winner, for Guelph. I did not find any Blackhawks trade rumors. The Athletic's Mark Lazerus wrote about the growing pains in year five of the rebuild.

The Blackhawks are back at the United Center tonight, listed for seven o'clock Central, hosting the very team we just talked about, the Kings. Los Angeles is only three, five and two, with eight points, one of the worst records in the league, but they are three and one on this five-game road trip, which ends in Chicago, and they have just beaten the Rangers by three. Panarin leads them with seven power-play goals, Doughty is rolling, and the Kings scored on their first two shots Monday night. Kuemper has been solid in goal. Los Angeles is thin up front with Zuccarello on injured reserve and Byfield day-to-day. I couldn't verify the Kings' exact record over their last ten games. After tonight, the Hawks have five of their next six games on the road, and a listing shows the next one Thursday at Detroit.

Now for the league leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with thirteen. Leon Draisaitl of Edmonton has twelve, and Artturi Lehkonen of Colorado has ten. Four players are tied for fourth with nine apiece: Auston Matthews, William Nylander, Alexis Lafreniere and Kyle Connor. In assists, Connor McDavid has twenty-six, miles ahead of everyone. Adam Fox of the Rangers has sixteen, Evan Bouchard and Nathan MacKinnon have fourteen each, and Jack Eichel and Quinn Hughes are tied for fifth with thirteen. In total points, McDavid leads with thirty-four. Draisaitl has twenty-four, and Kaprizov, Bouchard and MacKinnon are tied for third with twenty-one.

On to the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty points in thirteen games. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with nineteen points in just twelve games. In the Central, Minnesota leads with eighteen points in thirteen games, though Dallas has fifteen in only eleven games. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with eighteen points in twelve games. Carolina is first overall in the league with twenty points. Boston is second with nineteen, though the Bruins lead on points percentage, point seven nine two, thanks to the fewer games played. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with just five points in thirteen games. The Blackhawks are next to last with six points in eleven games. Then the Kings and Blue Jackets are tied at eight points, with Los Angeles having played ten games and Columbus nine, so Columbus has the better points percentage and the Kings would be third from the bottom.

That's your Tuesday morning rundown. Enjoy the day, and we'll see you tomorrow.
