Good morning, hockey fans. It's Tuesday, November third, and here's your rundown of Monday night in the NHL. It was a four game slate with one shootout, a Blackhawks loss that came with an injury to Patrick Kane, two first NHL goals in Detroit and one in Calgary, and a Bruins win that required Pastrnak in the shootout. Let's go game by game.

We start in Sunrise, where the Red Wings beat the Panthers five to three. Emmitt Finnie sealed it with an empty net goal in the final seconds, but the game winner belonged to Brandsegg-Nygard, who scored forty-five seconds after Faulk's power play goal early in the third period to make it four to two, and it was his first career game winner. Mazur opened the scoring with his first NHL goal, DeBrincat scored on a three on one rush, and Faulk and Brandsegg-Nygard each had a goal and an assist, while Seider picked up two assists and reached two hundred fifty career points. Tarasov made twenty-three saves for Detroit. For Florida, Reinhart extended his goal streak to three games, Ekblad scored short handed, and Bennett made it close late, but Markstrom, back from an upper body injury, made twenty-four saves in his first start since October seventeenth and the Panthers' three game winning streak ended.

In Boston, the Bruins beat the Golden Knights three to two in a shootout. Pastrnak was the only player to score in the shootout, going backhand to forehand past Hart, and that was the game winner. Regulation was a seesaw. Hagens scored in the first period, Geekie scored on the power play after Vegas forward Bowman was given a five minute major and a game misconduct for elbowing Poitras, and then Stone and Connelly answered for Vegas, with Connelly tying it early in the third. Swayman made twenty-one saves and Hart made twenty-five for Vegas. Marner had two assists to stretch his point streak to ten games, and the Bruins have now won six straight at home. Poitras did not return, and coach Sturm had no update on him.

In San Jose, the Flames beat the Sharks two to one. Stromgren scored the game winner in the third period, the first goal of his NHL career, after Klapka had given Calgary a first period lead. Sam Dickinson cut it to two to one later in the third, but San Jose could not find the equalizer. Dustin Wolf made twenty-six saves, and he is now eleven and two against the Sharks in his career. Askarov made twenty saves for San Jose, which has lost four of its last five, and coach Warsofsky said his team is struggling to generate scoring opportunities. Macklin Celebrini has gone six games without a goal.

And in Chicago, the Avalanche beat the Blackhawks five to two. Nazem Kadri scored twice, and his second, a power play goal in the second period that made it three to two, was the game winner. I'll cover this one in more depth in the Blackhawks section.

Now a few league headlines. First, confirmed news. There were no new completed trades reported last night. On the injury and discipline front, Pro Hockey Rumors reports that the NHL will not suspend Islanders forward Dylan Cozens for the hit that put Ducks forward A.J. Greer on injured reserve with an upper body injury. Detroit's Dylan Larkin remains suspended by the Red Wings. In roster moves, Pro Hockey Rumors reports the Islanders signed defenseman Ben Hutton to a professional tryout with Alexander Romanov and Ryan Pulock injured, the Maple Leafs placed Bo Groulx on waivers after Dakota Joshua returned, the Panthers sent goalie Cooper Black back to the minors with Markstrom healthy, and Blues defenseman Logan Mailloux began a conditioning stint in the minors after being out since late September. The trade deadline is Monday, March first, which is one hundred eighteen days away.

On the rumor side, all of this is rumor and not confirmed. Elliotte Friedman of Sportsnet reported that Nikita Kucherov told Tampa Bay he will not re-sign and wants a trade, and that the frustration was more about the pace of negotiations than the offers, which reportedly were a four year extension at twelve to thirteen million dollars a year and then a second offer near fifteen million. The Lightning have said they have no intention of trading him. Friedman also floated a Kucherov for Larkin swap with Detroit, saying it makes hockey sense. Darren Dreger of TSN said Toronto is not necessarily ruled out, but that Tampa's asking price would be steep, and that an offseason deal may be easier. Daily Faceoff also has a piece quoting Kucherov saying his request came from poor communication and respect.

Now the Blackhawks, who lost five to two at home to the Avalanche last night and are now four, ten and one. Chicago jumped out to a two to nothing lead. Wyatt Kaiser scored fifty-seven seconds in, the fastest game opening goal by a Blackhawks defenseman since March of twenty twenty-three, and rookie Roman Kantserov made it two to nothing on a two on one with Connor Bedard, his team leading sixth goal of the season. Then it fell apart. Kadri scored at fifteen twenty-three of the first and Jaden Schwartz tied it twenty-six seconds later. In the second, Bedard was sent off for holding, and Kadri scored on the power play, with Kadri saying he played a little Plinko in the crease. Schwartz made it four to two late in the period, Frank Nazar hit the crossbar and the post in the third, and Devon Toews added an empty netter. Spencer Knight made twenty-four saves, while Mackenzie Blackwood made twenty-one for Colorado. Bedard assisted on both Chicago goals and has a point in six straight games, and Kantserov has points in five straight. It was the second straight game the Hawks blew a multi goal lead, they have lost three in a row and six of their last seven, and Colorado has now won six straight against Chicago.

The big news is Patrick Kane, who left in the second period with an upper body injury and did not return. Coach Jeff Blashill said he is, quote, going to miss the next couple of games, for sure. Kane has nine points, one goal and eight assists, in fifteen games, second on the team behind Bedard. Daily Faceoff and Chicago Hockey Now both led with the injury, and Chicago Hockey Now said the loss leaves the Hawks in an even greater bind without him. Defenseman Bowen Byram also missed his eighth straight game with an upper body injury. I could only find Chicago Hockey Now's detailed take on the game, so I have no real consensus from the other Blackhawks outlets to report this morning, other than that the blown lead, and Kane's injury, are the two themes.

I could not retrieve the power play and penalty kill numbers from the NHL dot com team stats today, so I will skip special teams figures rather than quote another site.

On the schedule, the Blackhawks start a three game road trip tomorrow, Wednesday, November fourth, at Florida, with a seven thirty Eastern start, which is six thirty Central. They then play Thursday at Tampa Bay and Saturday at Carolina. Florida is eight, five and two with eighteen points in fifteen games, and went six, four and zero in its last ten, though it just had a three game winning streak snapped by Detroit. Reinhart has goals in three straight games, and Markstrom is back in goal after missing five games. I did not have a verified streak report beyond that for the Panthers.

Now the league leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with fifteen, Leon Draisaitl of Edmonton and Artturi Lehkonen of Colorado are tied with twelve, and Auston Matthews of Toronto and Kyle Connor of Winnipeg are tied with eleven. In assists, Connor McDavid leads with thirty, Evan Bouchard of Edmonton has eighteen, Leo Carlsson of Anaheim and Matthew Tkachuk are tied with seventeen, and Draisaitl has sixteen. In points, McDavid has thirty-nine, Draisaitl has twenty-eight, Bouchard has twenty-six, and Kaprizov and Nathan MacKinnon are tied for fourth with twenty-five.

Finally, the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty-six points, and the Hurricanes are first overall in the league by both points and points percentage. In the Central, Minnesota leads with twenty-three points. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with twenty-three points in sixteen games, though Montreal has twenty in fourteen games for a better points percentage. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with twenty-four points in fifteen games, ahead of Vegas with twenty. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with seven points, and Chicago is next to last with nine. Then there is a tie for third from the bottom at ten points between the Kings and the Blue Jackets, and the Kings have the worse points percentage because they have played thirteen games to Columbus' twelve.

That's your rundown. Have a great Tuesday, and we'll see you tomorrow.
