Good morning, hockey fans. It's Monday, November second, and here's your rundown of Sunday night in the NHL. It was a five game slate, all of it settled in regulation, with a record setting goalie in Carolina, a historic point streak in Edmonton, a big comeback in Anaheim, and a trade that came late last night. The Blackhawks were off, but they are back tonight. Let's go game by game.

We start in Raleigh, where the Hurricanes crushed the Jets seven to nothing. Sean Walker scored twice, including a short handed goal in the first period that stood up as the game winner. Juuso Valimaki, Sebastian Aho, Logan Stankoven, Mark Jankowski and Jesperi Kotkaniemi also scored, and Stankoven added two assists. Pyotr Kochetkov made twenty-six saves for his second shutout of the season and his tenth straight win to start the year, a franchise record, making him the third goalie in NHL history to win each of his first ten games of a season. For Winnipeg, Stuart Skinner was pulled in the second period after allowing five goals on seventeen shots, and the Jets have now lost nine straight.

In Washington, the Wild beat the Capitals five to one. Michael McCarron scored the game winner, a first period goal that made it two to nothing, after Quinn Hughes opened the scoring. Maxim Shabanov, Kirill Kaprizov into an empty net, and Danila Yurov on a five on three power play finished it late. Kaprizov's goal was his five hundredth career point, making him the second player in Wild history to get there, along with Mikko Koivu. Jesper Wallstedt made twenty-six saves, and Minnesota has now earned points in five straight games. Justin Sourdif had the lone goal for Washington, and Logan Thompson made twenty-four saves in a loss that snapped the Capitals' three game winning streak.

In Philadelphia, the Flyers beat the Mammoth six to three. Travis Konecny scored the game winner in the second period, and he had a goal and an assist in his return from a ten game absence. Matvei Michkov, Noah Cates, Christian Dvorak, Oliver Bonk and Cole Knuble, into an empty net, also scored, and Jamie Drysdale had three assists. Michkov now has ten goals in sixteen games, and Bonk's goal gave Philadelphia a season high five for a team that came in averaging the fewest goals per game in the league. Dan Vladar made nineteen saves. Dmitri Simashev, Mikhail Sergachev and Logan Cooley scored for Utah, but the Mammoth could not recover, and coach Andre Tourigny called it disappointing.

In New York, the Oilers beat the Rangers four to one. Colton Dach scored twice, including the game winner in the first period, and it was his first multigoal game in the NHL. Connor McDavid scored in the first minute, which extended his season opening point streak to fifteen games and his assist streak to fifteen games, an NHL record, and he became the fastest player to thirty assists, passing a mark Wayne Gretzky held. Evan Bouchard had a goal and two assists. Matt Tomkins, making his first NHL start since April of twenty twenty-four, stopped twenty-seven shots, and Edmonton is now seven and zero on the road. Pavel Dorofeyev scored for the Rangers, who have lost four straight, and Dylan Garand made twenty-one saves.

And in Anaheim, the Ducks beat the Senators six to three after trailing three to nothing. Ottawa jumped out front on goals from Philip Tomasino, Claude Giroux and Michael Amadio in the first period, but Anaheim scored six straight, four of them on the power play. Cutter Gauthier had a hat trick, and his one timer on a five on three at one fifty of the third was the game winner. Judd Caulfield added two goals and an assist, Roger McQueen scored on the power play, and Leo Carlsson had three assists. Laurent Brossoit made nineteen saves in his first start for Anaheim after being recalled from the minors. It is only the second time in franchise history the Ducks won by three or more after trailing by three or more. Jonas Johansson made twenty-seven saves for Ottawa, and coach Travis Green said the team would have been happy with the game if you took special teams out of it.

Now a few league headlines. First, confirmed news. The Nashville Predators and Seattle Kraken completed a one for one trade last night, sending Shane Wright to Nashville for Matthew Wood, a swap of former first round picks. Elliotte Friedman of Sportsnet was first to report it. The Capitals also acquired Remi Poirier from the Devils for a draft pick, according to Daily Faceoff. The Department of Player Safety fined Bruins defenseman Nikita Zadorov five thousand dollars, the maximum allowed, for roughing Blackhawks defenseman Alex Vlasic. Dylan Larkin remains suspended without pay by the Red Wings after walking away from the team, and his agent, Pat Brisson, said Larkin has had enough of the situation.

On the rumor side, Pro Hockey Rumors, citing David Pagnotta of The Fourth Period, is reporting that Nikita Kucherov wants a trade and is seeking about twenty million dollars a year, while Tampa's offers have reportedly been around fifteen million. That is a rumor and not confirmed, and the Lightning have said they have unequivocally no intention of trading him. Also from Pro Hockey Rumors, Drew Doughty hopes to finish his career with the Kings and has hired Pat Brisson to negotiate his next deal.

On injuries, the Avalanche announced defenseman Josh Manson will miss about eight weeks with an ankle injury. Daily Faceoff reports Islanders defenseman Ryan Pulock is out two to four weeks with an upper body injury, and Utah's Kailer Yamamoto is day to day. Coming back, the Flyers activated Konecny and Owen Tippett from injured reserve, and Utah activated Michael Carcone.

Now the Blackhawks, who did not play last night. The Blackhawks last game was a five to four overtime road loss against the Boston Bruins on Saturday night. Chicago plays tonight at seven p.m. Central at the United Center against the Colorado Avalanche.

On what the Blackhawks writers are saying, the shared theme is that the Hawks keep playing well enough to lead and not well enough to finish. Chicago Hockey Now's recap headline was that the Hawks blew multiple leads and could not close, Second City Hockey ran its recap under the headline Youth Overrided, and The Hockey Writers wrote about the overtime loss as a game that got away and also published a piece arguing the rebuild is now in year seven. Chicago Hockey Now also had a piece calling Anton Frondell's game a best effort and a new standard moving forward, so there is agreement that Frondell is a bright spot. That is about as much consensus as I can verify, since several of these pages only showed headlines.

I could not retrieve power play and penalty kill figures from the NHL dot com team stats, so I will skip special teams numbers today rather than quote another site.

On roster news, I did not find any confirmed Blackhawks lineup, injury or call-up news in the last day. Chicago Hockey Now's daily roundup mentioned Blackhawks NCAA prospects, and The Hockey Writers had a piece saying Nick Lardis' stint in Rockford is going to plan, but nothing there is a roster move.

Now tonight's opponent. Colorado is in the Central Division, sitting third in the division with seventeen points in fourteen games, tied on points with Dallas. The Avalanche are five, four and one in their last ten and have won two straight, including a five to two win Saturday in Calgary. They will be without Josh Manson. Artturi Lehkonen has a point in nine straight games and is tied for second in the league in goals, Cale Makar had four assists Saturday, and Nathan MacKinnon has twenty-four points. The Blackhawks are three, six and one in their last ten and have lost one in overtime in their most recent game.

Now the league leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with fifteen, Leon Draisaitl of Edmonton and Artturi Lehkonen of Colorado are tied with twelve, and Auston Matthews of Toronto and Kyle Connor of Winnipeg are tied with eleven. In assists, Connor McDavid leads with thirty, Evan Bouchard of Edmonton has eighteen, Leo Carlsson of Anaheim has seventeen, and four players are tied for fourth with sixteen, Draisaitl, Quinn Hughes, Matthew Tkachuk and Seth Jones. In points, McDavid has thirty-nine, Draisaitl has twenty-eight, Bouchard has twenty-six, and Kaprizov has twenty-five, with Matthews and Nathan MacKinnon tied for fifth at twenty-four.

Finally, the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty-six points, and the Hurricanes are first overall in the league by both points and points percentage. In the Central, Minnesota leads with twenty-three points. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with twenty-one points in fifteen games, though Montreal has twenty in fourteen games for a better points percentage. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with twenty-four points in fifteen games, ahead of Vegas with nineteen. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with seven points, and Chicago is next to last with nine. Then there is a tie for third from the bottom at ten points between the Kings and the Blue Jackets, and the Kings have the worse points percentage because they have played thirteen games to Columbus' twelve.

That's your rundown. Have a great Monday, and we'll see you tomorrow.
