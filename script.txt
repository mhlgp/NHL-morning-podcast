Good morning, hockey fans. It's Sunday, November fifteenth, and here's your rundown of Saturday night in the NHL. A big fourteen game slate, two of them went to a shootout, and the Blackhawks gave one away in a painful way. Let's go game by game.

We'll start in Chicago, where the Jets beat the Blackhawks five to four in a shootout. This one hurts. Chicago led four to nothing late in the second period on goals from Tyler Bertuzzi, Anton Frondell, Connor Bedard with four seconds left in the first, and Frank Nazar on the power play. Then Mark Scheifele and Kyle Connor scored less than two minutes apart, Cole Perfetti made it a one goal game early in the third on the power play, and Gabriel Vilardi tied it with just over a minute left with the goalie pulled. Scheifele scored in the shootout to win it. More on that in the Blackhawks section.

In Helsinki, the Hurricanes beat the Kraken five to one. ESPN has Andrei Svechnikov and Mark Jankowski each scoring twice, and Jordan Staal returning from injury, as Carolina swept the Global Series games. That's a nice way to bounce back from the long trip.

In Montreal, the Avalanche beat the Canadiens four to one, and they did it without Nathan MacKinnon and Cale Makar. Fedor Svechkov and Brock Nelson scored in the first and second, Nick Suzuki cut it to one, and Artturi Lehkonen's power play goal early in the third was the game winner. Lehkonen has scored in three straight games, and Nicolas Roy added the empty netter. Mackenzie Blackwood made twenty five saves, and Brent Burns played his sixteen hundredth game.

In Tampa, the Red Wings beat the Lightning two to nothing. Lucas Raymond scored the game winner in the second period, and Michael Brandsegg-Nygard added an empty netter. Daniil Tarasov made thirty one saves for his second shutout, and Detroit snapped a five game losing streak. It was Tampa's first home loss of the season, and coach Jon Cooper said Tarasov stole one.

In Boston, the Wild beat the Bruins six to two. Ryan Hartman and Danila Yurov each scored twice, Blake Coleman and Quinn Hughes added goals, and Coleman's early second period goal made it three nothing and was the game winner. Jesper Wallstedt made thirty saves, Brock Faber made his season debut after a skull fracture and was a plus four, and Kaprizov and Hughes both extended point streaks to eleven games. David Pastrnak and Fraser Minten scored for Boston, and Jeremy Swayman took the loss.

In Washington, the Devils beat the Capitals seven to four. Connor Hellebuyck made forty three saves, twenty three of them in the third period, to improve to seven and oh with New Jersey. Evan Rodrigues scored twice, and Timo Meier's power play goal late in the second period was the game winner. Luke Hughes had three assists, Jesper Bratt extended a point streak to eight games, and Anton Silaev made his NHL debut. Cole Hutson had three assists for Washington, which just couldn't hold anything in the third.

In Pittsburgh, the Senators beat the Penguins four to one. Claude Giroux scored late in the second to make it two to one, and that was the winner. Michael Amadio had a goal and an assist, Tim Stutzle scored, and Thomas Chabot hit the empty net. Linus Ullmark made sixteen saves for his two hundredth career win. Pittsburgh managed just seventeen shots and went scoreless on three power plays.

In St. Louis, the Panthers beat the Blues four to one. Brady Tkachuk scored on the power play, and Dmitry Kulikov made it two nothing less than a minute and a half later, which was the game winner. Anton Lundell and Sam Reinhart added goals, and Jacob Markstrom made twenty three saves. Reinhart's assist on the first goal was the seven hundredth assist in NHL history. Dylan Holloway got one back late for the Blues.

In Columbus, the Flyers beat the Blue Jackets four to three. Denver Barkey scored twice, including the game winner in the third period, which was his first multi goal game in the NHL. Matvei Michkov had a breakaway goal, Trevor Zegras scored, and Joseph Woll made twenty eight saves. Columbus has lost four straight, and coach Rick Bowness missed a second straight game with a health issue.

In Toronto, the Oilers beat the Maple Leafs one to nothing. Evan Bouchard scored the only goal in the second period, a one timer off a pass from Connor McDavid. Devon Levi made nineteen saves for his first NHL shutout. McDavid's season opening point streak is now twenty one games. Toronto lost its sixth straight, and a John Tavares goal was waved off for a high stick early in the third.

In Dallas, the Stars beat the Sabres two to nothing. Jamie Benn scored late in the second period for his thousandth career point, and Wyatt Johnston added a late power play goal. Jake Oettinger made twenty two saves for his fourth shutout of the season. Alex Lyon was good for Buffalo with twenty eight saves, but the Sabres went oh for five on the power play.

In Utah, the Sharks beat the Mammoth six to five in a shootout. Dmitry Orlov had a goal and three assists, and his third period goal made it five to four, but Barrett Hayton tied it for Utah on the power play late. Macklin Celebrini and Mason Marchment scored in the shootout. Zac Jones scored twice for Utah, Eric Pohlkamp scored his first NHL goal, and the Mammoth have now lost five straight.

In Calgary's game in Los Angeles, the Flames beat the Kings three to two. Matvei Gridin scored the game winner midway through the third, Morgan Frost and Yegor Sharangovich, into the empty net, added the others, and Dustin Wolf made twenty nine saves. Mats Zuccarello scored with under a minute left, but it wasn't enough.

And in Anaheim, the Islanders beat the Ducks six to two. Simon Holmstrom had his first NHL hat trick, and Ben Hutton's early goal in the first period was the game winner. Matthew Schaefer had three assists, a career high, and Mathew Barzal returned from a knee issue. Cutter Gauthier scored for Anaheim, extending his goal streak to eight games. The bad news for New York is that Bo Horvat and Logan Stanley both left the game and didn't return, according to reports.

Now a few league headlines. The trade deadline is Monday, March first, which is one hundred six days away. I didn't see any newly completed trades. On the injury front, Pro Hockey Rumors says Nathan MacKinnon is expected to miss at least a month and Cale Makar about two weeks. Montreal's Chris Kreider is expected to miss six to eight weeks. Dallas's Miro Heiskanen missed his fourth straight game, and the Stars put Lian Bichsel on injured reserve. Mammoth coach Andre Tourigny's team is still without Logan Cooley and several others. In rumor land, Pro Hockey Rumors is reporting that Ottawa is looking to move Andre Burakovsky, but his salary makes a deal tough. That's a rumor, nothing is done. Red Wings general manager Shawn Horcoff has said Detroit won't take weak offers for Dylan Larkin, and TSN's Pierre LeBrun has reported the Blackhawks are scouring the market for help. Both of those are reporting, not done deals.

Now the Blackhawks. They lost five to four in a shootout to Winnipeg, and it's a rough one. They fell to five, thirteen and two, and they've now lost seven of their last eight. The first half of the game was the best they've looked in a while. Bertuzzi's goal snapped a twelve game drought, Bedard had a goal and an assist, and Patrick Kane's assist on Bedard's goal moved him to fourteen hundred ten career points, passing Dale Hawerchuk for twenty third on the all time list. Frank Nazar scored on the power play, Teuvo Teravainen and Sam Rinzel each had two assists, and Spencer Knight made twenty seven saves. Winnipeg's Stuart Skinner gave up three goals on eight shots and got pulled for Nico Daws, who was excellent the rest of the way. Then it fell apart. Ian Cole took a holding the stick penalty that led to Perfetti's power play goal, Vilardi tied it with the goalie pulled, and Bedard hit the post in overtime. Kane scored in the shootout, but rookie Roman Kantserov was stopped, and Scheifele and Connor finished it. Scheifele also became the Jets franchise leader in points, passing Hawerchuk. Tyler Bertuzzi left early in the third with an undisclosed injury, and coach Jeff Blashill didn't have an update. Blashill said his team got on its heels way too much after checking well early. Around the Hawks world, the feel is that this was another same old story, a team that can't close out a lead, with defensive breakdowns and a blue line that doesn't help Knight enough, though the fans and writers did like how the top line and the power play looked early. Chicago Hockey Now's trade buzz and LeBrun both have the Blackhawks reportedly looking for help, but that's a rumor, not a done deal. Bleacher Report also lists Bowen Byram on injured reserve and Dillon Boucher recalled from Rockford.

I wasn't able to pull the power play and penalty kill numbers this morning, so I'll leave those out.

In points, McDavid has fifty. Draisaitl has thirty eight, Kaprizov has thirty seven, Bouchard has thirty four, and Nathan MacKinnon and Quinn Hughes are tied for fifth with thirty.
