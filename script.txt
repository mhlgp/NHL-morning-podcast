Good morning, hockey fans. It's Wednesday, October twenty-eighth, and here's your rundown of Tuesday night in the NHL. Eleven games hit the ice, and we've got some wild ones, including a comeback for the ages in Anaheim. Let's go game by game.

We start in Philadelphia, where Columbus beat the Flyers four to three in overtime. The Blue Jackets raced out to a three nothing lead in the first period, with Adam Fantilli, Kent Johnson and Mateychuk all scoring inside of three minutes in the first period. Philadelphia clawed back with goals from Cates, Trevor Zegras, and then Drysdale with just eleven seconds left in regulation to force overtime. But Johnson had the last word, scoring his second of the night at two twenty-two of overtime for the game winner. The Flyers earned a point but sit near the bottom of the Metropolitan Division.

In Winnipeg, Florida cruised past the Jets five to one. Carter Verhaeghe scored twice on the power play, Niko Mikkola's goal at eight forty-two of the first period was the game winner, Sam Reinhart added a power play goal in the second, and Brady Tkachuk closed the scoring in the third. Winnipeg's only goal was a shorthanded one from Lowry in the second period. The Panthers' power play was the story, converting three times, and the Jets are now four, five and three on the year.

In Nashville, the Islanders beat the Predators four to three in a shootout. New York built a three to nothing lead on goals from Anthony Duclair, Schenn, and Bo Horvat. Nashville stormed back, with Filip Forsberg on the power play, Kerfoot, and then Ryan O'Reilly tying it at three early in the third. Neither side could break through in overtime, and Palmieri's shootout goal delivered the extra point for the Islanders. Semyon Varlamov and Juuse Saros both had to be sharp, but Nashville keeps piling up overtime and shootout losses, with five on the season.

In Raleigh, Carolina edged Boston two to one. Boston's Mark Zacha opened the scoring on the power play early in the first, but Jackson Blake tied it with under a minute left in the first period, and Sebastian Aho scored the game winner at ten nineteen of the third. Pyotr Kochetkov was the difference in net for the Hurricanes, who remain the best team in the league by record. Jeremy Swayman took the loss for the Bruins in a tight, low-scoring game.

Out in Chicago, the Blackhawks beat the Kings two to one, snapping a three game losing streak. Samuel Helenius scored first for Los Angeles at two thirty-one of the first period.
