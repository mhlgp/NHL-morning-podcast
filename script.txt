Good morning, hockey fans. It's Friday, October twenty-third, and here is your rundown of Thursday night in the NHL. Twelve games were on the board, and plenty of them were worth staying up for. We'll go game by game, then hit the headlines, the Blackhawks, the leaderboards, and the standings.

We start in Boston, where the Bruins blanked Nashville six to nothing. Casey Mittelstadt's opening goal was the game winner. David Pastrnak scored twice and finished with two goals and two assists. Elias Lindholm on the power play, J J Peterka and Mark Kastelic also scored. Michael DiPietro stopped twenty-eight shots for his first career shutout, one day after his first career win. Nashville coach Andrew Brunette called it the team's worst effort of the year, by far, and said too many players were passengers.

In Toronto, the Maple Leafs beat the Rangers five to four in a wild one. Troy Stecher scored the winner with twenty-eight seconds left in regulation, on a delayed penalty, and that goal gave Toronto its fifth, so it was the game winner. Brandon Duhaime had a goal and two assists, Teddy Blueger had a goal and an assist, and Sergei Bobrovsky made twenty-one saves for career win number four hundred fifty-nine. New York got goals from Mika Zibanejad, Bjorkstrand, Kartye, and Alexis Lafreniere, but let this one slip at the very end. The Leafs have won three in a row.

In Ottawa, the Sharks rolled over the Senators six to two for their first regulation win of the season. Collin Graf scored and added an assist, Yaroslav Askarov stopped thirty-nine shots, and Graf's second period shorthanded goal made it three to one, which stands as the game winner. Tyler Toffoli and Jacob Trouba also scored, and two empty netters sealed it. For Ottawa, the bigger blow may be goalie Linus Ullmark going on injured reserve with a lower body strain, one to two weeks.

In New Jersey, the Devils beat the Ducks four to one. Arseny Gritsyuk and Nico Hischier scored in the first period, Nico Daws made sixteen saves, and Hischier's power play goal was the winner. Anthony Mantha scored in the third and Jack Hughes added an empty netter. Anaheim has now lost five in a row.

In Pittsburgh, the Penguins shut out the Panthers four to nothing. Nicholas Robertson scored twice, including an empty netter, Evgeni Malkin and Hendrix Lapierre each had a goal and an assist, and Arturs Silovs made twenty-six saves for his second shutout of the year. Robertson's first goal opened the scoring and was the game winner. Florida, with Cooper Black, who at six foot nine is the tallest goalie in league history, making his debut, had no shots at all during a four minute power play. Coach Paul Maurice questioned his team's attitude. That ended a four game Pittsburgh losing streak.

On Long Island, the Kings beat the Islanders three to two in overtime. Artemi Panarin scored twice in the first period, and Brandt Clarke won it at two forty-three of overtime. Darcy Kuemper made forty-one saves as the Kings were outshot forty-three to twenty-six. William Eklund scored on the power play and Schaefer tied it in the third for New York.

In Dallas, the Stars edged the Jets three to two in overtime. Roope Hintz scored the tying goal with two minutes left in regulation, then won it on a breakaway at three forty-seven of overtime. Wyatt Johnston scored and assisted, and Jason Robertson reached five hundred career points. Stuart Skinner made thirty-five saves for Winnipeg, which got goals from Gabriel Vilardi and Kyle Connor but has dropped four straight. Dallas has won four in a row, though Radek Faksa left with an injury.

In St. Louis, the Blues pounded the Golden Knights six to two. Dylan Holloway had two goals and an assist, and McTavish's goal in the second period was the game winner. Justin Barron had a four point night, and Joel Hofer made twenty-seven saves. Vegas has now lost back to back games by six to two. Jack Eichel and Brett Howden scored, and Mitch Marner missed the game for the birth of his second child.

In Calgary, the Flames beat the Wild two to one. Aydar Suniev and Joel Farabee both scored on the power play in the first period, and Farabee's, his first of the season, was the game winner. Devin Cooley made thirty-six saves, even as Minnesota outshot Calgary seventeen to three in the third. Michael McCarron got the lone Wild goal, and Kirill Kaprizov's seven game point streak ended.

In Edmonton, the Oilers beat the Hurricanes six to five in a shootout. Connor McDavid and Leon Draisaitl each had two goals and three assists, five points apiece. McDavid tied it with about ninety seconds left, and Ryan Nugent-Hopkins, playing his first game of the season, scored the only shootout goal. Carolina got goals from Seth Jarvis in his season debut, Sebastian Aho, Jackson Blake, Logan Stankoven and Valimaki, but took five penalties, and coach Rod Brind'Amour said that was the problem.

In Seattle, the Kraken beat the Mammoth five to three. Utah's Logan Cooley scored twice to tie it at three, but Samoskevich scored the winner late in the third, and Gaudreau added an empty netter. Philipp Grubauer made thirty-three saves.

And in Vancouver, the Red Wings beat the Canucks five to two. Lucas Raymond scored twice, and his power play goal in the first period was the game winner. John Gibson left after two periods with an eye issue not believed to be serious, and Michal Postava made seven saves in his NHL debut. Vancouver has lost nine straight, and Brock Boeser called the effort embarrassing.

Now the headlines. On the confirmed side, the Penguins lost Bryan Rust for six weeks, and Canadiens captain Nick Suzuki is out two to four weeks. Sharks forward Igor Chernyshov will be out a while with an upper body injury after blocking a shot. The Rangers lost goalie Igor Shesterkin for the season. The league fined Senators forward Nick Cousins and Panthers forward Matthew Tkachuk after Wednesday's action. Now the rumors. Elliotte Friedman has said Connor Hellebuyck's team suspension in Winnipeg ends around October twenty-third, though other outlets say next week, and a report cited by Reuters says at least three teams are involved in talks. That is all rumor, nothing is done. Darren Dreger of TSN is reporting that Pittsburgh's budget may shape its trade plans and that the club is shopping veterans. David Pagnotta of The Fourth Period says Carolina's extension talks with defenseman Jalen Chatfield are paused. Several outlets link Detroit's Dylan Larkin to trade talk, but nothing says a deal is close. And Pro Hockey Rumors flagged that the Flyers may include Travis Konecny in trade talks. Again, rumors.

Next, the Blackhawks, who did not play last night. They are three and six, last in the Central. Their last game was a four to nothing home loss to Dallas, which ended a two game winning streak that included a four to three overtime win over Winnipeg on a Levshunov goal. Patrick Kane has set the record for most assists by a U.S. born player, and Connor Bedard is back in the lineup. Earlier, Roman Kantserov scored his first NHL goal and Teuvo Teravainen had three points against Pittsburgh. Dominic Toninato was sent to Rockford. Bowen Byram is day to day with an upper body injury, and coach Jeff Blashill said he is trending in the right direction, per Charlie Roumeliotis of WGN Radio. Chicago ranks near the bottom in goals scored and allowed. No Blackhawks rumors worth reporting today.

The Hawks play tonight at the United Center against the Montreal Canadiens, puck drop around seven Central. Montreal is four, three and two, with ten points, in the Atlantic, and lost to San Jose four to three in overtime in its last game. With only nine games played, that is also its record over the last ten. Suzuki is out. That is a good chance for Chicago, whose power play sits around twelve percent. Then it's Florida Sunday, the Kings Tuesday, at Detroit Thursday, and at Boston next Saturday.

League leaders. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov leads with eleven, Draisaitl has ten, Kyle Connor nine, and a five way tie at eight: McDavid, Auston Matthews, Mark Stone, Alexis Lafreniere, and Artturi Lehkonen. In assists, McDavid has twenty-three, Adam Fox fourteen, Jack Eichel thirteen, Evan Bouchard and Matthew Tkachuk twelve each. In points, McDavid has thirty-one, Draisaitl twenty-one, Bouchard and Eichel nineteen, and Matthews eighteen.

Standings. Carolina leads the league with eighteen points, though Edmonton has seventeen in only ten games and leads in points percentage. Carolina leads the Metropolitan, Boston the Atlantic with seventeen, Edmonton the Pacific with seventeen, and the Central is tied between Utah and Minnesota at fourteen. At the bottom, Vancouver has five points, last in the league, and then four teams are tied at six: Columbus, Los Angeles, Calgary, and Chicago, with Chicago and Calgary having played nine games to the other two's eight.

That's your rundown. Have a great Friday.
