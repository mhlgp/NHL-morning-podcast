Good morning, hockey fans. It's Thursday, November fifth, and here's your rundown of Wednesday night in the NHL. It was a short four game slate, with a Kempe hat trick in Los Angeles, a late rally in Buffalo, an overtime winner in Seattle, and another loss for the Blackhawks in Florida. Let's go game by game.

In Sunrise, the Panthers beat the Blackhawks five to three. Sandis Vilmanis had the best night of his young career with two goals and two assists, his first multipoint game in the NHL, and his second goal at eleven minutes of the third was the game winner. Florida's top line of Verhaeghe, Reinhart and Vilmanis combined for nine points, with Reinhart adding a goal and two assists, and Verhaeghe scoring on the power play and getting an assist. Seth Jones also scored against his old team. Chicago actually got back into it late, with Frank Nazar scoring a short handed breakaway goal and then another one to make it four to three, but Reinhart sealed it with an empty netter. Spencer Knight made twenty five saves, and Jacob Markstrom made seventeen for Florida. More on the Hawks in a bit.

In Buffalo, the Senators beat the Sabres two to one. Peyton Krebs scored for Buffalo in the second on a deflection, and that held up until late in the third, when Shane Pinto tied it on a rebound and Michael Amadio scored the game winner with a one timer at sixteen fifty one. Tim Stutzle set that one up with a poke check that started the rush. Colten Ellis made twenty eight saves for the Sabres, including one pad stop people are calling a save of the season candidate, and Samuel Ersson made twenty three for Ottawa. It ended a three game winning streak for Buffalo, and coach Lindy Ruff said his team got outworked and outsmarted.

In Los Angeles, the Kings crushed the Sharks seven to two. Adrian Kempe came back from illness and had a hat trick plus an assist, and Quinton Byfield scored twice on the power play. Byfield's first one, late in the first period, was the game winner. Erik Haula and Joel Armia also scored, and Artemi Panarin had four assists. The Kings went three for five on the power play, and Darcy Kuemper made thirty saves. For San Jose, Macklin Celebrini and Ivar Stenberg scored on the power play, but Yaroslav Askarov gave up seven, and the Sharks held a players only meeting afterward. That's three straight losses for them.

And in Seattle, the Kraken beat the Flames two to one in overtime. Yan Sharangovich put Calgary up in the first, Berkley Catton tied it in the second, and Matty Beniers won it on a breakaway less than a minute into overtime. Joey Daccord made twenty five saves for Seattle, and it was the Flames' first overtime loss of the year.

Now a few league headlines. First, the confirmed news. Pro Hockey Rumors says Brad Lambert cleared waivers after Winnipeg put him on the wire, per Elliotte Friedman, and the Blue Jackets placed Emil Andrae and Danton Heinen on waivers. On the injury front, Detroit's John Gibson left Tuesday's game with a lower body injury and the Red Wings called up goalie Michal Postava on an emergency basis. Dallas's Miro Heiskanen is day to day with an upper body injury, and Ottawa's Ridly Greig skipped warmups with an undisclosed injury. Senators forward Hayden Hodgson is back from a two game suspension for a reckless hit on Brandt Clarke. I didn't see any new completed trades.

On the rumor side, all of this is rumor and not confirmed. Pro Hockey Rumors is passing along a report from TSN's Darren Dreger that teams are calling Tampa general manager Julien BriseBois about Nikita Kucherov, who has reportedly asked for a trade, and the Lightning have said they don't plan to move him. Pro Hockey Rumors also has the Blue Jackets listening to offers for Dmitri Voronkov, and NBC Sports Boston is wondering about Boston and Dylan Larkin, though Pro Hockey Rumors says the Bruins aren't on his ten team trade list. The trade deadline is Monday, March first, which is one hundred sixteen days away.

Now the Blackhawks, who lost five to three in Florida on Wednesday night and have dropped four straight. They're four and eleven and one, and one and six and one over their last eight. Florida has now won ten in a row against Chicago at home. The good news is Frank Nazar's two goals, including that short handed breakaway, and rookie Roman Kantserov scoring his seventh of the year to stretch his point streak to six games, which is a Blackhawks rookie record pace for the last forty years, matching eight rookies. Connor Bedard and Alex Vlasic had... actually, Bedard and Del Mastro each had two assists, and Del Mastro's shot is the one that Kantserov poked in. Chicago got outplayed early, fell behind two nothing, and a neutral zone turnover led to the first goal. Ian Cole's turnover led to the third. Coach Blashill said some guys competed hard but a couple gave away free goals, and that a few players played at the end of their stick. Bedard said they shot themselves in the foot.

The feel around the team is pretty rough. Writers and fans keep saying the Bedard, Kantserov and Bertuzzi line is the one consistent group, and that Knight is getting hung out to dry by a defense that turns the puck over and doesn't clean up the front of the net. Plenty of people are also questioning the coaching and wondering if the front office makes a move soon.

I wasn't able to pull the power play and penalty kill numbers from the NHL stats page this morning, so I'll leave those out.

On the roster, Patrick Kane left Monday's game with an upper body injury. Blashill said he'd miss at least a couple of games, and one report has him possibly back as soon as Saturday in Carolina, while ESPN's injury page lists November twelfth. Bowen Byram is on injured reserve, but the move is backdated, and Blashill says he isn't medically cleared. Ryan Ellis is out for the season with a back injury.

Chicago plays tonight at Tampa Bay, at seven Eastern, six Central. The Lightning are nine and three and two, with twenty points, atop the Atlantic standings in games played terms but behind Boston in points, and they're a perfect seven and oh at home. They're six, two and two in their last ten and beat Detroit four to two on Tuesday. Nikita Kucherov leads the team with twenty points, Brandon Hagel has sixteen with eight goals, Jake Guentzel also has eight goals, and Brayden Point has thirteen points. Gourde and Girgensons are out. After that, the Hawks are at Carolina Saturday and home Thursday against Buffalo.

Now the leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov leads with fifteen. Leon Draisaitl and Kyle Connor are tied for second with thirteen, Artturi Lehkonen has twelve, and Auston Matthews has eleven. In assists, Connor McDavid is way out in front with thirty one. Evan Bouchard has nineteen, Nathan MacKinnon and Matthew Tkachuk have eighteen, and four guys are tied for fifth with seventeen: Leo Carlsson, Quinn Hughes, Clayton Keller and Adam Fox. In points, McDavid has forty. Draisaitl has twenty nine, Bouchard twenty eight, MacKinnon twenty seven, and Kaprizov and Matthews are tied for fifth with twenty six.

Last, the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty six points. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with twenty five. In the Central, Minnesota leads with twenty five, and in the Atlantic, Boston leads with twenty three. Carolina is first overall by points with twenty six, but Edmonton has twenty five in one fewer game, and leads the league by points percentage. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with seven points, Chicago is next with nine, and then it's a four way tie at twelve points between Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Los Angeles and Columbus, with Philadelphia having the worst points percentage of that group.

That's your rundown. Have a good Thursday.
