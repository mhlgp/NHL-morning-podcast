Good morning, hockey fans. It's Wednesday, November fourth, and here's your rundown of Tuesday night in the NHL. It was an eleven game slate, and it had everything: the biggest comeback in NHL history in Montreal, a second straight hat trick for Cutter Gauthier, a Jason Robertson natural hat trick in Washington, and a seven goal blowout in Columbus. The Blackhawks were off, but they hit the road tonight. Let's go game by game.

We start in Montreal, where the Jets beat the Canadiens five to four in overtime, and honestly, this one needs a minute. Winnipeg trailed four to nothing going into the third period. Then Tyrel Bauer scored his first NHL goal with under three minutes left, Morgan Barron made it four to two, and Kyle Connor scored twice on one timers with the goalie pulled, the second one with thirty-three seconds left to tie it. That's four goals in two minutes and six seconds, the fastest four goals in franchise history, and it's the largest comeback in NHL history. Dylan Samberg won it in overtime, off a takeaway and a feed from Adam Lowry. Cole Caufield had two power play goals for Montreal and Jacob Fowler made forty-two saves, but Caufield called the collapse inexcusable. Winnipeg had been on a nine game losing streak before this.

In Newark, the Devils beat the Oilers four to three in a shootout. Jack Hughes scored two goals forty-five seconds apart in the second period, and Arseny Gritsyuk scored in the fourth round of the shootout for the win, with Connor Hellebuyck stopping Kapanen to seal it. Hellebuyck made eighteen saves in his first start at the Prudential Center and has won three straight. Edmonton got goals from Bouchard and Nugent-Hopkins, plus a five on three power play goal from Draisaitl early in the third. Connor McDavid extended his season opening point streak to sixteen games, though he had only one shot. It was Edmonton's first road loss of the season in regulation or overtime, after seven straight road wins.

In Tampa, the Lightning beat the Red Wings four to two. Brayden Point scored twice in the first period, his first multigoal game of the year, and Kucherov set up both of them. Cirelli added a goal, and McDonagh scored an empty netter in the final second. Cirelli's goal was the game winner by the usual counting rule. Vasilevskiy made nineteen saves. Detroit got goals from Raymond and Compher, but John Gibson left after the first period with a lower body injury, and Tarasov stopped twelve in relief. Compher said only about thirty minutes of the game was good enough.

In Washington, the Stars beat the Capitals six to five. Dallas jumped out to a four to nothing lead on a natural hat trick from Jason Robertson, three goals in five minutes and twenty seconds in the second period. Washington's Logan Thompson was pulled after four goals on eleven shots. The Capitals fought all the way back, with Kyrou, Jenner, Chychrun, Sourdif and Leonard all scoring, but Roope Hintz's goal early in the third made it six to three and turned out to be the game winner. Hintz had three points, and Rantanen scored for his eight hundredth career point. The scary part for Dallas is that Miro Heiskanen left with an upper body injury and will be reevaluated today.

In Philadelphia, the Wild beat the Flyers four to two. Boldy and McCarron scored thirty-one seconds apart in the first period, and Eriksson Ek made it three to nothing early in the second, which was the game winner. Yurov added an empty netter. Wallstedt made twenty-three saves, and Minnesota's point streak is now six games. Kaprizov has nine points during that run. Philadelphia missed a season high twenty-two shots, and coach Tocchet said they had too many missed opportunities.

In Pittsburgh, the Golden Knights beat the Penguins three to two. Tomas Hertl scored twice, including the winner on the power play with thirty-five seconds left in the third. Connelly tied it earlier on a breakaway, and Adin Hill made twenty-three saves. Mitch Marner stretched his point streak to eleven games. Pittsburgh went zero for six on the power play, and it didn't even register a shot during a one minute twenty-two second five on three. The Penguins have lost three straight, and Malkin missed the game with a lower body injury.

In Toronto, Utah beat the Maple Leafs three to two. Nick Schmaltz scored the game winner on the power play, poking in a rebound with about seven minutes left after Bobrovsky stopped a Keller one timer. Guenther and Peeke also scored, Cossa made twenty-seven saves, and Keller had three assists. Jack Roslovic scored twice for Toronto, and Bobrovsky made thirty-three saves, but Matthews said the team lacked energy early, and the Leafs are one and three in their last four.

In Anaheim's visit to Vancouver, the Ducks beat the Canucks five to four. Cutter Gauthier had his second straight hat trick, and he now has eight goals in three games. His third goal early in the third was the game winner. Granlund had a goal and two assists, Carlsson scored short handed, and Beckett Sennecke had a career high four assists. DeBrusk scored twice in the third for Vancouver and nearly pulled off the comeback, but the Canucks have lost twelve of their last fourteen, and coach Malhotra said the effort was too little, too late.

In Columbus, the Blue Jackets crushed the Avalanche seven to two. Columbus scored twenty-eight seconds in, got two from Charlie Coyle, and had a big night from Olivier, Sillinger and Coyle, who combined for nine points. Jake Christiansen's goal early in the second made it three to one, and that was the game winner by the usual counting rule. Avalanche goalie Scott Wedgewood allowed four goals on nine shots before being pulled, and Colorado's three game winning streak ended. Makar said it just seems like when one thing goes wrong, everything goes wrong.

In Nashville, the Predators beat the Hurricanes four to one. Perbix, Josi and Edstrom scored, and Colton added an empty netter, and Josi's goal early in the second was the game winner by the usual counting rule. Saros made twenty-nine saves. Kochetkov's ten game winning streak to start the season ended, and Carolina coach Brind'Amour said his team was trying to do too much. Shane Wright played in his first game with Nashville after the trade. Marchessault missed the game and is day to day.

In New York, the Rangers beat the Blues three to one. Adam Fox scored the opener, Bjorkstrand made it two to nothing in the third, and Tolvanen added an empty netter. Bjorkstrand's goal was the game winner. Dylan Garand made thirty-two saves for his first win since October nineteenth, and the Rangers killed all six Blues power plays. Pius Suter scored for St. Louis with fifty-two seconds left, but the Blues couldn't get enough traffic in front of the net, according to coach Montgomery.

Now a few league headlines. First, the confirmed news. Dallas has Heiskanen's upper body injury and Detroit has Gibson's lower body injury, both to watch. Pro Hockey Rumors says Winnipeg put forward Brad Lambert on waivers, per Elliotte Friedman, and the Maple Leafs' Bo Groulx cleared waivers. Frank Seravalli reported Bruins forward Braeden Bowman wasn't hit with any further discipline for the elbow on Matthew Poitras, who missed practice. The Kings' Alex Turcotte is back in full practice, with no return timetable. Off the ice, Pro Hockey Rumors says Canadian officials and several European countries are pushing back on Russia competing at the World Juniors, after the arbitration court ruled the IIHF had to let them in. The trade deadline is Monday, March first, which is one hundred seventeen days away.

On the rumor side, all of this is rumor and not confirmed. Pro Hockey Rumors has a poll on where Nikita Kucherov could end up, and it says Emily Kaplan reported he wants to play with another star. The Lightning have said they have no intention of trading him, and Kucherov had two assists last night.

Now the Blackhawks, who were off last night. The Blackhawks last game was a five to two home loss against the Colorado Avalanche on Monday night. The mood around the team is pretty grim. The two things that keep coming up are the blown early leads and the coaching. Fans and local writers keep pointing at the team falling apart after a good start, and the Chicago Sun-Times reported that a handful of fans tried to start a fire Blashill chant late Monday, with lots of empty seats. National outlets like Daily Faceoff and The Hockey News have Jeff Blashill on their early season hot seat lists, though most don't expect a quick firing. The Sun-Times says the four day break after this road trip could be the moment for GM Kyle Davidson to make changes, and the trip is against Florida, Tampa and Carolina without Patrick Kane and Bowen Byram. There's also a lot of talk about how the lineup and special teams have been handled, and about whether the young guys like Bedard and Frondell are growing.

On the roster, Chicago recalled forward Dillon Boucher from Rockford and placed Byram on injured reserve with an upper body injury. Pro Hockey Rumors says Byram's move is procedural, and Ben Pope of the Sun-Times reports he's seeing a specialist. Nick Lardis was the preferred call up, but he has an undisclosed injury, and per Scott Powers of The Athletic, Boucher is unlikely to play while Kane is out. Kane left Monday's game with an upper body injury, and coach Blashill said he'll miss at least the next couple of games.

I wasn't able to pull the power play and penalty kill numbers from the NHL dot com team stats today, so I'll skip special teams figures rather than quote another site.

Tonight the Blackhawks play at Florida, with a seven thirty Eastern start, which is six thirty Central. Chicago is four, ten and one, and one, seven and two in its last ten, and previews have them winless on the road so far. The Panthers are eight, five and two with eighteen points in fifteen games, in the middle of the Atlantic pack, and they're six and four in their last ten. They had a three game winning streak snapped Monday by Detroit. Sam Reinhart has goals in three straight, and Jacob Markstrom, back from an upper body injury, is listed as the likely goalie. Aleksander Barkov and Brad Marchand are on injured reserve. After that, Chicago plays Thursday at Tampa Bay and Saturday at Carolina.

Now the league leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with fifteen, Leon Draisaitl of Edmonton and Kyle Connor of Winnipeg are tied with thirteen, Artturi Lehkonen of Colorado has twelve, and Auston Matthews of Toronto has eleven. In assists, Connor McDavid leads with thirty-one, Evan Bouchard of Edmonton has nineteen, Nathan MacKinnon has eighteen, and Leo Carlsson of Anaheim, Quinn Hughes of Minnesota and Matthew Tkachuk are tied with seventeen. In points, McDavid has forty, Draisaitl has twenty-nine, Bouchard has twenty-eight, MacKinnon has twenty-seven, and Kaprizov and Matthews are tied for fifth with twenty-six.

Finally, the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty-six points, which is first overall in the league by points. Edmonton has twenty-five in just sixteen games, which is the best points percentage in the league. In the Central, Minnesota leads with twenty-five points. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with twenty-three points. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with twenty-five, ahead of Vegas with twenty-two. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with seven points, Chicago is next to last with nine, and the Kings are third from the bottom with ten points in just thirteen games.

That's your rundown. Have a great Wednesday, and we'll see you tomorrow.
