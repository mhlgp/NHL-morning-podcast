Good morning, hockey fans. It's Thursday, October twenty-ninth, and here's your rundown of Wednesday night in the NHL. It was a lighter slate, just four games, but we had a Kraken comeback finish, a record for Connor McDavid, and a big third period in Colorado. Let's go game by game.

We start in Seattle, where the Kraken beat the Maple Leafs five to four in regulation, and it was a wild finish. Seattle had a two goal lead, but Toronto fought back, and Auston Matthews tied it with a goal at eighteen thirty of the third period. Then Brandon Montour won it with thirty-two seconds left, a slap shot from the right circle that went through Sergei Bobrovsky's pads. That was the game winner. Chandler Stephenson had two goals and an assist for Seattle, and Berkly Catton, Josh Mahura and Ryker Evans also scored. Eighteen year old Gavin McKenna was the story for Toronto, with two power play goals for his first multi goal game in the NHL, and Matthews added a goal and two assists. Bobrovsky finished with only twelve saves, and that last one will sting. Seattle is now eight and four and one.

In Washington, the Capitals beat the Flyers five to two. Alex Tuch scored twice, including an empty netter, and Jordan Kyrou scored twice, one on the power play. The game winner was Aliaksei Protas, who made it three to one late in the second period. Dylan Strome had three assists, and defenseman Jack St. Ivany, claimed off waivers from Winnipeg, made his Washington debut with two assists. For Philadelphia, Sean Couturier scored his first of the season, and Matvei Michkov added a power play goal in the third, but the Flyers have now lost three straight and sit at three, eight and four. Joseph Woll took the loss in net, and Logan Thompson made twenty-three saves for Washington.

In Edmonton, the Oilers shut out the Flames four to nothing. Mathieu Joseph scored the game winner on Edmonton's very first shot of the game, at seven forty-six of the first period. Mattias Ekholm, back from a concussion, scored off the crossbar, and Kasperi Kapanen and Alex Formenton scored seconds apart early in the third period. Tristan Jarry made twenty-four saves for his first shutout of the season. Connor McDavid had two assists, which stretched his assist streak to start the season to thirteen games, an NHL record, passing Wayne Gretzky and others at twelve. It was a rough night for Calgary. Goalie Devin Cooley left with a lower body injury after a crease collision, and Brennan Othmann also left with an upper body injury. Dustin Wolf came in and stopped twenty of twenty-three.

And in Denver, the Avalanche beat the Devils five to two with four unanswered goals in the third period. Connor Brown scored a shorthanded goal in the first for New Jersey, and Gabriel Landeskog tied it on the power play in the second. Then early in the third Kadri scored, followed by Kelly, and Kelly's goal was the game winner. Nathan MacKinnon added a breakaway goal and Schwartz finished it, though Connor Brown cut it to five to two late with his second of the night. Brent Burns reached nine hundred fifty career points, tenth most all time among defensemen. Colorado did lose defenseman Josh Manson to a lower body injury in the first period, and he'll miss time. Mackenzie Blackwood played his three hundredth NHL game for Colorado and made twenty saves, while Jake Allen faced a heavy load of shots for New Jersey and stopped thirty-nine.

Now a few league headlines. Some confirmed news first. In Seattle, Pro Hockey Rumors says Jared McCann is expected to miss ten weeks, and in Ottawa, William Eklund has a torn ankle tendon and is out until at least January. In Toronto, Kirill Marchenko is expected to miss a few weeks with a lower body injury, according to TSN's Mark Masters. In Detroit, Marco Kasper and Mason Appleton are a couple of weeks away, and Albert Johansson will miss tonight's game. I did not find any new suspensions.

On the rumor front, and these are only reports, not deals. Pierre LeBrun reports the Sharks are among the teams interested in Detroit restricted free agent Simon Edvinsson, though Detroit would prefer to re-sign him before the December first deadline. Bruce Garrioch of Postmedia reports Ottawa general manager Steve Staios has been calling around for forward help, and Blackhawks forward Teuvo Teravainen was floated as one possibility, though the Senators may wait closer to the deadline. Pierre LeBrun also quotes Calgary general manager Craig Conroy as saying moving Zach Whitecloud would take a significant offer, and Pro Hockey Rumors says Ryan Strome is likely to be traded before the March first deadline. That deadline is a little over four months away.

Now the Blackhawks. Chicago did not play Wednesday. Their most recent game was Tuesday night at the United Center, a two to one win over the Kings that snapped a three game losing streak and ended an oh for seventeen power play drought. Roman Kantserov tied it on the power play and Wyatt Kaiser scored the winner in the third, with Spencer Knight making twenty-five saves. The Hawks are four, eight and zero, and the local coverage was mostly relief. Chicago Hockey Now and Second City Hockey both framed it as a needed win, and the power play goal got the most attention. Both also pointed to the lines being shuffled, with Chicago Hockey Now crediting a third line that came alive after a slow start. The only consensus I'd put on it is that the Hawks need more of that effort and more offense, because that was a one goal game. There wasn't enough overlap among the writers for me to give you more than that.

On special teams, the figures I found were from before the Kings game and may lag. A BetMGM ranking as of October twenty-sixth had Chicago last in the league on the power play at eight point three three percent, and Kalshi had the penalty kill at seventy-six point five percent through eleven games, while BetMGM earlier had it at seventy-five percent, around twenty-fourth. Different sites show somewhat different rankings, and Kantserov's goal will bump the power play number up a little, so treat those as approximate.

On roster news, Chicago Hockey Now headlines say the Hawks are looking for a specific need on the trade market, and that Chicago is among the teams reported to have interest in Capitals winger Ivan Miroshnichenko. That is a rumor, and I could not see the details. I found no new call-ups or injuries, and Andrew Mangiapane was activated from injured reserve earlier this month.

Chicago plays tonight at Detroit, with puck drop at six thirty Central at Little Caesars Arena. It's the first meeting of the season. The Hawks are zero, four and zero on the road and four and six in their last ten, and Patrick Kane is one assist away from nine hundred for his career. Detroit is five, four and three in the Atlantic with thirteen points, and they are five, two and three in their last ten, with a one game win streak. On Monday they beat Edmonton four to three in a shootout, in goalie Michal Postava's first NHL start, and Dylan Larkin scored with one minute and seventeen seconds left to force overtime.
