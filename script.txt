Good morning, hockey fans. It's Monday, October twenty-sixth, and here is your rundown of Sunday night's NHL action. Five games were on the board, and we'll take them one at a time.

We start with the Heritage Classic in Winnipeg, where the Montreal Canadiens beat the Jets two to one at Princess Auto Stadium. Lane Hutson got Montreal on the board early in the second period, and Kaiden Guhle scored the game winner a few minutes later. Jacob Fowler was the story in the Canadiens net, turning aside nearly everything the Jets threw at him. Isak Rosen scored late in the third period for Winnipeg to make it interesting, but Stuart Skinner and the Jets could never get the equalizer.

Next, the Dallas Stars beat the New York Islanders six to three. The Stars scored the first goal on Tyler Seguin, and Jason Robertson then added two goals of his own. Justin Hryckowian scored on the power play, and Wyatt Johnston scored the game winner early in the third period. Mikko Rantanen put the finishing touch on it late. The Islanders got goals from rookie defenseman Matthew Schaefer, Kyle Palmieri and Brayden Schenn, but Ilya Sorokin gave up six goals and could not hold the Stars back.

In Nashville, the Predators routed the Colorado Avalanche six to two. Filip Forsberg opened the scoring on the power play, and Steven Stamkos and Ryan O'Reilly each scored twice. Jack Drury scored the game winner late in the second period. Nashville went to the power play over and over and cashed in four times. Colorado's Scott Wedgewood had a rough night in goal, and the Avalanche got goals from Artturi Lehkonen and Devon Toews, but never really threatened.

In Minnesota, the Wild beat the Vancouver Canucks four to two. Maxim Shabanov scored on the power play late in the first, Bobby Brink made it two, and Quinn Hughes, facing his old team, scored the game winner on the power play. Kirill Kaprizov added a goal early in the third. Max Sasson and Brock Boeser scored for Vancouver in the third period, but it was too little too late for a Canucks team that sits last in the league.

And the Chicago Blackhawks lost to the Florida Panthers three to two at the United Center. We'll dig into that one in a minute. Lars Eller scored the game winner late in the third period.

Now the broader league headlines. First, the confirmed news. The Winnipeg Jets traded goalie Connor Hellebuyck to the New Jersey Devils last week, getting Dawson Mercer, Nico Daws, a prospect and picks in return, according to Pro Hockey Rumors. The Carolina Hurricanes re-signed forward Alexander Nikishin to a one year deal worth three million dollars, ending his standoff, and Sportsnet's Elliotte Friedman was first on the story. The Senators extended head coach Travis Green for four years, and the Bruins signed Marat Khusnutdinov for three years. On the injury front, the Kraken's Jared McCann is expected to miss ten weeks, Penguins forward Bryan Rust is out about six weeks, and Canadiens captain Nick Suzuki is out two to four weeks. Penguins forward Benjamin Kindel collapsed on a flight home from Utah, was placed on injured reserve, and was alert afterward. The Daily Faceoff did not list any new suspensions.

Now some rumors, and I want to be clear that these are rumors, not confirmed. Pro Hockey Rumors is reporting that the Blue Jackets are listening to offers for Dmitri Voronkov, and that the Capitals have given Ivan Miroshnichenko permission to seek a trade. Pro Hockey Rumors also says about ten teams are interested in Red Wings defenseman Simon Edvinsson, though the price tag may be steep. It also reports that Predators general manager Chris MacFarland may sell veterans like Ryan O'Reilly if Nashville falls out of the race. And ESPN's Emily Kaplan says free agent Patrik Laine is not looking for offers overseas and remains open to the NHL.

Now to the Blackhawks. Chicago fell three to two to the Panthers, and drops to three wins and eight losses, with only six points. Ryan Greene scored just over two minutes in to give Chicago an early lead, but Sandis Vilmanis tied it for Florida, and Matthew Tkachuk put the Panthers ahead on the power play late in the first. Roman Kantserov tied it in the second period with his fourth goal of the season, which leads the team. But Lars Eller scored the winner with just over two minutes left in the third. Spencer Knight took the loss in goal for Chicago. Akira Schmid was in net for Florida. Bleacher Nation noted that defenseman Korchinski was back in the lineup and highlighted prospect goals in the system, and the team called it a tight contest.

On the roster side, Bowen Byram is skating again after an upper body injury, and coach Jeff Blashill said it is not a concussion. Andrew Mangiapane was activated from injured reserve on Friday. Connor Bedard has played only five games this season, after being activated from injured non-roster on October thirteenth, and has two goals and three assists. Patrick Kane leads Chicago with seven points, Anton Frondell has six, and Teuvo Teravainen, Tyler Bertuzzi and Bedard each have five. Dominic Toninato was assigned to Rockford last week. I did not find any Blackhawks specific rumors from the last day or two.

Chicago plays again Tuesday night, October twenty-seventh, at home against the Los Angeles Kings, with the puck dropping at seven Central. The Kings are not in great shape either. They are two wins, five regulation losses and two overtime losses, good for six points in nine games, tied with Chicago in points, and they lost one to three in New Jersey on Saturday after a win in Washington and an overtime win on Long Island earlier in the week. Their last ten is really their whole season so far, since they have played only nine. Artemi Panarin has the hot hand with six goals and nine points in nine games, Adrian Kempe has eight points, and Alex Laferriere and Brandt Clarke each have seven. Goalie Darcy Kuemper is two, three and one with a save percentage of point eight nine. After that, the Hawks visit Detroit on Thursday, and Boston on Saturday.

On to the leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with thirteen, Leon Draisaitl of Edmonton has twelve, Artturi Lehkonen of Colorado has ten, and then three players are tied for fourth with nine each: Auston Matthews, Alexis Lafreniere and Kyle Connor. In assists, Connor McDavid leads with twenty-five, Adam Fox has sixteen, Evan Bouchard and Nathan MacKinnon each have fourteen, and Jack Eichel and Quinn Hughes are tied for fifth with thirteen. In total points, McDavid is way out in front with thirty-three, Draisaitl has twenty-three, and Kaprizov, Bouchard and MacKinnon are tied for third with twenty-one each. Matthews is next with nineteen.

Finally, the standings. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with nineteen points in twelve games. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty points in thirteen games. In the Central, Minnesota leads with eighteen points in thirteen games, and in the Pacific, Edmonton and Vegas are tied with seventeen points, though Edmonton has played two fewer games, eleven to thirteen. Overall, Carolina is first in the league with twenty points, while Boston has nineteen points in one fewer game, and actually leads the league in points percentage. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with five points in thirteen games, and Chicago and Los Angeles are tied at six points, with Los Angeles having played just nine games to Chicago's eleven.

That's your rundown. Enjoy your Monday, and we'll see you tomorrow.
