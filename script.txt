Good morning, hockey fans. It's Saturday, October twenty fourth, and here is your rundown of Friday night in the NHL. It was a light night, just two games on the board, and one of them was in Chicago.

We start at Ball Arena, where the Colorado Avalanche beat the Tampa Bay Lightning six to three. Colorado jumped out early with goals from Devon Toews at one oh eight of the first and Nazem Kadri on the power play, his first of the season, and Fedor Svechkov made it three to nothing early in the second. Jake Guentzel pulled Tampa Bay back into it with a power play goal, but Artturi Lehkonen restored the three goal cushion with the game winner in the second period. Nathan MacKinnon then added a goal and three assists, his second four point night in a week, and he now has twenty points in eleven games. Guentzel scored twice for the Lightning and Charle Edouard D'Astous cut it to five three late, but Martin Necas iced it into an empty net, and Mackenzie Blackwood stopped thirty one shots for the win. Tampa Bay's Nikita Kucherov was held without a point and remains one short of passing Steven Stamkos as the franchise's all time points leader.

Over in Chicago, the Montreal Canadiens beat the Blackhawks four to one at the United Center. Connor Bedard gave Chicago the lead at four twenty three of the first period, but Jake Evans tied it before the intermission, and then Evans put Montreal in front with a short handed goal in the second period that turned out to be the game winner. Kaiden Guhle made it three to one late in the second with Josh Anderson screening Spencer Knight, and Mike Matheson sealed it with an empty netter. Jacob Fowler made twenty saves for Montreal, who played without the injured Nick Suzuki, while Knight made twenty four saves for Chicago. The Blackhawks went zero for five on the power play, and more on that in a moment.

Now for the league headlines. First, the big one, and please note this is all rumor and reporting at this point. The Hockey Writers, citing Darren Dreger and Elliotte Friedman, says Winnipeg goalie Connor Hellebuyck is the subject of a rapidly developing trade situation. Dreger posted that it was a busy day developing, and Friedman said New Jersey and Winnipeg were likely working on a deal, with a possible third team involved. Carolina, Buffalo, Utah, Los Angeles and San Jose have also been linked, and the Rangers could possibly help facilitate a three team deal. I was not able to confirm that any trade has been completed, so treat it as unconfirmed. The same report says Hellebuyck's suspension from the Jets was ending, and that Winnipeg reportedly wanted to avoid having him rejoin the team.

Also from the rumor mill, the Hockey Writers' rumor roundup says Detroit captain Dylan Larkin has reportedly submitted a list of ten teams he would accept a trade to, and Pro Hockey Rumors columnist Lyle Richardson says he remains linked to Montreal. Trade speculation also continues around Philadelphia's Travis Konecny.

On the injury front, Oilers defenseman Mattias Ekholm will miss at least a week after a collision with Carolina's William Carrier in which he appeared to hit his head. Canadiens captain Nick Suzuki is out two to four weeks with an upper body injury. Senators goalie Linus Ullmark was placed on injured reserve, and Tampa Bay's Anthony Cirelli left Friday's game with an undisclosed injury. And in Vancouver, Brock Boeser said after a five to two loss to Detroit that he doesn't know where the disconnect is and called the team's struggles embarrassing, per Sportsnet. He has a full no movement clause, which would complicate any trade.

Now to the Blackhawks. Chicago is now three and seven on the season, has lost two straight and four of its last six, and sits last in the Central Division with six points. They came out of a five day break and started well, with Bedard scoring his second goal of the season off a pass from Patrick Kane. But Montreal tied it late in the first, and after that the Hawks gave up chances. Bedard said they allowed two or three two on one rushes, including the one that led to the short handed goal, and said those are mistakes they can't be giving up. Chicago's power play going zero for five was the other big story. There was one milestone, as defenseman Ian Cole played his one thousandth NHL game, joining Ryan McDonagh as the second defenseman from the two thousand seven draft to reach that mark. On the roster front, Chicago activated Andrew Mangiapane from injured reserve, while defenseman Bowen Byram missed his third straight game with an upper body injury. I did not find any Blackhawks specific trade rumors in the last couple of days.

Chicago is back home Sunday afternoon at two o'clock Central against the Florida Panthers, then hosts the Kings on Tuesday night. Florida is a bit of a surprise, sitting sixth in the Atlantic at five, four and two, with twelve points, and they have lost two straight. Over their last ten they are four, four and two. Matthew Tkachuk leads the team with fifteen points, three goals and twelve assists in eleven games, Seth Jones has eleven points, and Brady Tkachuk has seven goals. One report says Aleksander Barkov and Brad Marchand are on injured reserve, and that goalie Jacob Markstrom is out one to two weeks, though I'd double check that closer to game time.

Now the leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with eleven, followed by Edmonton's Leon Draisaitl with ten. Colorado's Artturi Lehkonen and Winnipeg's Kyle Connor are tied for third with nine, and Connor McDavid and Auston Matthews are tied for fifth with eight each. In assists, McDavid has twenty three, which is remarkable, then Adam Fox with fourteen, Nathan MacKinnon and Jack Eichel with thirteen each, and Evan Bouchard and Matthew Tkachuk tied for fifth with twelve. In total points, McDavid leads with thirty one, Draisaitl has twenty one, MacKinnon twenty, Bouchard and Eichel are tied for fourth with nineteen, and Matthews has eighteen.

Finally, the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with eighteen points, and that is also the most in the league. Edmonton has seventeen points in only ten games, so the Oilers lead the league in points percentage at point eight five zero. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with seventeen points, ahead of Tampa Bay, Ottawa and Toronto at fourteen. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with seventeen, one ahead of Vegas with sixteen. The Central is tight, with Utah and Minnesota tied at fourteen points, and Dallas at thirteen with a game in hand on them in points percentage terms.

At the bottom, Vancouver is last with just five points. Then there is a four way tie at six points, made up of Chicago, Calgary, Columbus and Los Angeles. Chicago has played ten games, Calgary nine, and Columbus and Los Angeles only eight each, so the Hawks have the lowest points percentage of that group at point three zero zero.

That's your Saturday morning rundown. Enjoy the games.
