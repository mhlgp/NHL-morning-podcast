Good morning, hockey fans. It's Tuesday, November tenth, and here's your rundown of Monday night in the NHL. It was a quiet three game slate, with Connor McDavid keeping his point streak alive in Edmonton, a shootout thriller in San Jose, and the Flyers finally snapping a skid in Vancouver. The Blackhawks were off. Let's go game by game.

In Edmonton, the Oilers beat the Rangers three to one. McDavid opened the scoring at six forty two of the first, pouncing on an errant pass from the Rangers goalie behind the net. Leon Draisaitl scored twice, a wrister through Dylan Garand's legs in the second and a shorthanded empty netter late, and the second goal was the one that sealed it, though the game winner was really the second Edmonton goal, Draisaitl's first, at two fifty nine of the second. Jaroslav Chmelar had cut it to two to one with a shorthanded goal for New York. Devon Levi made twenty eight saves and is eight and one, and Draisaitl called him the main reason they won. McDavid's point streak to start the season is now nineteen games, the first streak that long since Dany Heatley in two thousand five, though his eighteen game assist streak ended. The Rangers had a handful of two on ones and couldn't finish, and Garand made seventeen saves in the loss.

In Vancouver, the Flyers beat the Canucks three to one. Michael Bunting scored in the first, just two minutes in, and it was his first goal of the season. He'd been a healthy scratch five straight games before moving back to the top line. Travis Konecny made it two nothing on the power play in the second, and that was the game winner. Lekkerimaki got one back on the power play in the third, and Travis Sanheim hit a shorthanded empty netter at nineteen oh one. Joseph Woll made eighteen saves, twelve of them in the third period when Vancouver outshot Philly thirteen to two. Leevi Merilainen made fourteen saves for the Canucks, who have now lost six straight, all in regulation, and coach Manny Malhotra said their start was the most frustrating part and that it's becoming a problem.

And in San Jose, the Sharks beat the Islanders three to two in a shootout. Ben Hutton and Victor Eklund, on the power play, gave New York two leads, but Ivar Stenberg tied it in the first and Tyler Toffoli tied it in the second, off a deflection that hit Adam Pelech in the face. Nobody scored in the third or overtime, and Stenberg scored the only shootout goal, which was the game winner. Yaroslav Askarov stopped all eight shootout shots he faced and made thirty saves before that, while Ilya Sorokin made thirty seven saves in a loss. San Jose ended a five game losing streak, and Alexander Wennberg came back after missing ten games. The Islanders have lost four straight.

Now the Blackhawks. The Blackhawks last game was a nine to two road loss to the Carolina Hurricanes on Saturday night. As for the feel around the team, writers at Blackhawk Up, Chicago Hockey Now and Yardbarker keep coming back to the same things. A lot of the talk is about Roman Kantserov and Connor Bedard being the bright spots, and about whether Anton Frondell is being used right in a checking role.

Names floated by outlets include Owen Tippett, Ivan Miroshnichenko, Dmitri Voronkov, Rickard Rakell, Drew O'Connor and Nino Niederreiter. Again, that's rumor, nothing is done.
