Good morning, hockey fans. It's Sunday, November first, and here's your rundown of Saturday night in the NHL. It was a ten game slate with one overtime finish, one shootout, a Blackhawks loss that stung, a season debut and a first goal for a couple of young players, and a Red Wings captain situation that has gone from messy to official. Let's go game by game.

We start in Boston, where the Bruins beat the Blackhawks five to four in overtime. Elias Lindholm got the game winner at two thirty-two of overtime, fending off two defenders and tucking a forehand past Arvid Soderblom. Chicago had a four to two lead late in the third and was about ten seconds from its first road win of the year, but Mason Lohrei made it four to three, and Morgan Geekie tied it with a one timer with the goalie pulled and just twelve seconds left. Anton Frondell scored twice for the Hawks, Ryan Greene and Connor Bedard also scored, and Michael DiPietro stopped thirty-seven shots for Boston. David Pastrnak scored his four hundred twenty-eighth goal, moving past Patrice Bergeron into third on the Bruins all time list. I'll come back to this one in the Blackhawks section.

In Detroit, the Blues beat the Red Wings five to four. The game winner belonged to Aleksanteri Kaskimaki, who scored his first NHL goal in his eleventh game, redirecting an Alexey Toropchenko shot at thirteen fifteen of the third period, and he called it pretty unreal. Justin Barron had a goal and an assist, including the one timer that tied it at four, and Jimmy Snuggerud also had a goal and an assist. Joel Hofer made twenty-four saves. For Detroit, Lucas Raymond had a goal and an assist and Pavel Buchnevich scored, but John Gibson gave up five goals on fifteen shots, though coach Todd McLellan defended him and said he had no chance on the first two.

In Nashville, the Predators beat the Lightning five to four in a shootout. Steven Stamkos was the hero, scoring the game tying power play goal early in the third and then the only goal of the shootout. Justus Annunen made twenty-two saves and stopped all four Tampa Bay shootout tries, and Reid Schaefer scored the opening goal, his first of the season. For Tampa Bay, Nikita Kucherov had three assists in his first game since the trade request news, and John Carlson had a goal and an assist, with Andrei Vasilevskiy making twenty-nine saves in the loss.

The Oilers blanked the Islanders four to nothing. Ryan Nugent-Hopkins scored twice, and his goal in the second period was the game winner. Tristan Jarry stopped eighteen shots for his second straight shutout. Connor McDavid had an assist to stretch his season opening point streak to fourteen games, Leon Draisaitl had two assists, and Vasily Podkolzin and Jason Dickinson, into an empty net, also scored. Ilya Sorokin made twenty saves for the Islanders.

In San Jose, the Senators routed the Sharks five to one. Drake Batherson scored the game winner in the second period and extended his point streak to seven games. Dylan Cozens had a goal and an assist, Tim Stutzle had a goal and an assist, Ridly Greig scored short handed, and Shane Pinto added an empty netter. Samuel Ersson made twenty-two saves for his first win with Ottawa. Michael Misa scored the lone goal for San Jose, but Sharks coach Ryan Warsofsky said his team was slow all night and got outworked all over the ice.

In Los Angeles, the Sabres beat the Kings three to two. Tage Thompson scored the game winner on the power play late in the third, off a Jack Quinn pass, and it was his second of the night, which moved him past Rene Robert for ninth on the Sabres all time goals list. Quinn also scored and Ukko-Pekka Luukkonen made twenty-seven saves, with Buffalo going two for three on the power play after a one for nineteen slump. Anton Forsberg made twenty-eight saves for the Kings, who fell to one and four at home, and coach Peter Laviolette said they could not find that next goal.

In Montreal, the Canadiens edged the Penguins five to four. Josh Anderson had the game winner in the second period, a Lane Hutson point shot that deflected off Kris Letang's leg, and Anderson finished with two goals and an assist. Jacob Fowler made twenty-five saves for his seventh straight win, and Chris Kreider had a goal and an assist. For Pittsburgh, Sidney Crosby had a goal and two assists, tying Marcel Dionne for sixth in career points at seventeen seventy-one, and Erik Karlsson also had a goal and two assists. Sergei Murashov made twenty-two saves in defeat.

In Vancouver, the Maple Leafs beat the Canucks four to two. Auston Matthews scored the game winner early in the second period and has now picked up a point in eight straight road games. William Nylander scored on an early breakaway after Jamie Oleksiak got caught flat footed, and Anthony Stolarz made twenty-six saves. Brock Boeser and Max Sasson scored for Vancouver, but the Canucks are now three, twelve and one, and one and eleven and one in their last thirteen.

In Dallas, the Stars beat the Blue Jackets three to one. Thomas Harley got the game winner, a shot that beat Jet Greaves with one second left in the first period to make it two to nothing, after Mikko Rantanen opened the scoring on the power play. Jake Oettinger made twenty-four saves, and Arttu Hyry had a goal and an assist, sealing it with an empty netter. Damon Severson scored for Columbus, which has lost two straight, and had a third period goal from Valeri Nichushkin waved off for offside.

And in Denver, the Avalanche beat the Flames five to two. Artturi Lehkonen scored twice, including the game winner in the second period that made it three to nothing, and he has a point in nine straight games. Cale Makar had four assists, only the fourth defenseman in NHL history with four points on Halloween, and Nathan MacKinnon and Martin Necas also had goals. Mackenzie Blackwood made twenty-five saves. Arsenii Sergeev made twenty-eight saves in his season debut for Calgary, and defenseman Jake Middleton left late in the third with no update given.

Now a few league headlines. First, confirmed news. The Detroit Red Wings suspended Dylan Larkin without pay on Thursday after he told the team he will no longer play for them, and he will sit until he is traded. Interim general manager Shawn Horcoff said, quote, we are disappointed by his decision, and that he is open to all different kinds of deals. NHL dot com reports that other clubs have called with interest, that any trade needs Larkin's approval, and that Larkin has five seasons left on an eight year, sixty-nine point six million dollar contract. Pro Hockey Rumors also lists the Blue Jackets as listening to offers for Dmitri Voronkov, and that is a rumor from a headline only. On the Kucherov situation, the Lightning had already said they have no intention of trading him, and he played last night.

On injuries, Pro Hockey Rumors reports Ottawa's Andre Burakovsky and Warren Foegele are expected to miss three to four weeks, according to coach Travis Green. Oilers forward Isaac Howard was hit knee on knee and helped off the ice, though the coach said he seems to have avoided serious injury. Kings forward Adrian Kempe is day to day, and the Minnesota Wild are without both Marcus and Nick Foligno. In roster news, the Ducks waived Nick Jensen, per Elliotte Friedman, the Stars activated Esa Lindell and are expected to give David Perron his debut, the Maple Leafs activated Chris Tanev off injured reserve, and the Blue Jackets activated Ivan Provorov. The trade deadline is Monday, March first, which is one hundred twenty days away.

Now the Blackhawks, who lost five to four in overtime at Boston last night, and the story is how close they were. Chicago blew leads of one to nothing and two to one, then went up four to two before the Bruins scored three straight. Frondell was the headliner, with a power play goal about ten seconds into the first man advantage, one in the second period, and two more good looks at a hat trick, including in overtime. Blashill moved Frondell onto the top power play unit with Bedard, Patrick Kane and Tyler Bertuzzi, and Kane's primary assist on that first goal was the nine hundredth of his career, making him the first American born player to reach the mark. Bedard added a breakaway goal and an assist, his point streak is now five games, and Soderblom, in his first start in twenty-three days, allowed five goals on thirty-two shots. Chicago had forty-one shots, a season high, and Bedard called it the team's best game of the year but said they need to find a way to win. The Blackhawks are now four, nine and one, and zero, five and one on the road.

On what the Blackhawks writers are saying, the consensus is that the effort was real but the results are not, and the pressure on Blashill keeps growing. Chicago Hockey Now's headline was that the Hawks blew multiple leads and could not close, The Hockey Writers called it a competitive effort with no finish and wrote that Blashill's job security is slipping, and Second City Hockey earlier this week wrote that Blashill could be in danger if results do not change. The other shared point is that Frondell on the top power play unit gave the Hawks a spark. Chicago Hockey Now also flagged Nikita Zadorov's punch on Alex Vlasic, which drew no power play and could draw a look from the league.

I could not retrieve power play and penalty kill figures from the NHL dot com team stats, so I will skip special teams numbers today rather than quote another site.

For lineup news, Bedard returned from injury, and The Hockey Writers notes Kevin Korchinski was a healthy scratch.

The Blackhawks play next tomorrow, Monday, November second, at seven p.m. Central at the United Center against the Colorado Avalanche. Colorado is eight, five and one with seventeen points, and has won two straight, including last night's five to two win in Calgary. The Avs are five, four and one over their last ten. Artturi Lehkonen has a nine game point streak and has thirteen points in that stretch, Cale Makar had four assists last night, and Nathan MacKinnon has twenty-four points. After that, Chicago plays at Florida on Wednesday, at Tampa Bay on Thursday, and at Carolina on Saturday.

Now the league leaderboards. In goals, Kirill Kaprizov of Minnesota leads with fourteen, Leon Draisaitl of Edmonton and Artturi Lehkonen of Colorado are tied with twelve, and Auston Matthews of Toronto and Kyle Connor of Winnipeg are tied with eleven, with William Nylander and Tage Thompson right behind at ten. In assists, Connor McDavid leads with twenty-nine, and five players are tied for second with sixteen, Evan Bouchard, Matthew Tkachuk, Quinn Hughes, Seth Jones and Adam Fox. In points, McDavid has thirty-seven, Draisaitl has twenty-six, and Kaprizov, Matthews and Nathan MacKinnon are tied with twenty-four, with Bouchard next at twenty-three.

Finally, the standings. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty-four points, and the Hurricanes are first overall in the league by both points and points percentage. In the Central, Minnesota leads with twenty-one points. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with twenty-one points in fifteen games, though Montreal has twenty in fourteen games for a better points percentage. In the Pacific, Edmonton leads with twenty-two points in fourteen games, ahead of Vegas with nineteen. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with seven points, and Chicago is next to last with nine. Then there is a three way tie for third from the bottom at ten points, Philadelphia, Columbus and Los Angeles, and Philadelphia has the worst points percentage of that group because it has played fifteen games.

That's your rundown. Have a great Sunday, and we'll see you tomorrow.
