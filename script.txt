Good morning, hockey fans. It's Sunday, October twenty fifth, and here is your rundown of Saturday night's NHL action. Twelve games were on the schedule, and we'll go through every one of them.

We start in Philadelphia, where the Ducks beat the Flyers five to four. The Flyers jumped out to a two goal lead on a pair of first period power play goals from Michkov and Martone. Anaheim came back with Leo Carlsson, Greer and Caulfield, with Foerster adding a short handed goal for Philadelphia in between, and then Luneau put the Ducks ahead early in the third. Sennecke's power play goal made it five to three and turned out to be the game winner, with Carlsson in the middle of it. Michkov scored again to make it close, but the Flyers could not find the equalizer, and the loss ended their three game winning streak.

In New Jersey, the Devils beat the Kings three to one. Laughton gave Los Angeles a one nothing lead in the second period, but Mantha tied it with under two minutes left in the frame, and Brett Pesce's goal early in the third was the game winner. Gritsyuk iced it into an empty net. Jake Allen was solid in goal for the Devils, and the Kings fell to two and five and two.

Pittsburgh edged Nashville three to two in overtime. Steven Stamkos gave the Predators a first period lead, Dewar tied it for the Penguins early in the second, and Struble put Nashville back ahead before Rickard Rakell tied it with about a minute left in regulation. Koivunen won it for Pittsburgh in overtime, scoring the game winner at three forty four of the extra frame.

Washington beat Buffalo four to three in overtime. Alex Tuch scored twice, including the game winner in overtime, and Dylan Strome also scored twice for the Capitals. Buffalo got goals from Quinn, Doan and Ostlund, and a point out of it, but let a chance at two slip away.

Columbus beat Vegas two to one in overtime. Mateychuk opened the scoring for the Blue Jackets, Hertl tied it for Vegas late in the first, and then Cole Fantilli won it for Columbus on the power play less than a minute into overtime.

Ottawa buried the Rangers seven to four. Drake Batherson had a hat trick, with the last goal into an empty net, and Stutzle, Cozens, Sanderson and Amadio also scored. Sanderson's third period goal was the game winner. New York got goals from Cuylle, Miller, Lafreniere and Dorofeyev, but trailed most of the night and could never get over the hump.

Boston beat San Jose six to four. The game was tied three times before the Bruins scored three times in the third period, with David Pastrnak and James Hagens scoring within about forty seconds of each other, and Minten's power play goal was the game winner. Borgen added an empty netter. San Jose got goals from Misa, Sherwood, Orlov and Smith, but could not hold on.

Toronto routed Edmonton seven to two. William Nylander scored twice, and one of them was his three hundredth career goal. Auston Matthews scored, Stecher's second period goal was the game winner, and the Leafs also got goals from MacEwen and Lettieri along with a fluky first period tally. Anthony Stolarz stopped thirty shots. Leon Draisaitl scored twice for Edmonton, pushing his league leading total to twelve, but it was a rough night for the Oilers overall.

Carolina blanked St. Louis four to nothing. Walker scored the game winner early in the second period, Kotkaniemi scored twenty seconds later, and Stankoven and Ehlers added goals as well. It was a dominant night for the Hurricanes, who now lead the league in points.

Utah beat Tampa Bay five to four in overtime. It was a wild one. Tampa got goals from Holmberg, Point and Geekie, but Utah kept coming back with Yamamoto, Guenther and Lee. Defenseman Maxim Simashev scored twice, tying it late in the second period and then winning it just under two minutes into overtime.

Calgary beat Detroit four to one. Joel Farabee had a goal and two assists, Parekh's power play goal in the second period was the game winner, and Ryan Strome and Gridin added goals for the Flames. Copp had the lone goal for Detroit.

Minnesota beat Seattle five to one. Kirill Kaprizov scored just twenty six seconds in, Coleman's second period goal was the game winner, and Maatta, Eriksson Ek and an empty netter from Coleman finished it off. Catton had the Kraken's only goal.

Now the Blackhawks. A quick note: Chicago did not play Saturday. Their last game was Friday night at the United Center, where the Canadiens beat them four to one. Connor Bedard opened the scoring at four twenty three of the first, on a feed from Patrick Kane, for his seventy seventh career goal. Jake Evans tied it late in the first, then scored a short handed goal on a two on one rush early in the second, one that Bedard said should not have happened. Kaiden Guhle made it three to one late in the second, and Mike Matheson added an empty netter. Chicago went zero for five on the power play, and Spencer Knight made twenty four saves. It was also defenseman Ian Cole's one thousandth NHL game. The Blackhawks are three and seven and have lost two in a row.

On the roster front, defenseman Bowen Byram has missed three straight games with an upper body injury and will not play today. Forward Andrew Mangiapane was activated from injured reserve Friday and is expected to play. Arvid Soderblom could get the start in goal after Knight started five straight. Ryan Ellis is out for the season with a back injury.

Chicago plays this afternoon at the United Center against Florida, with puck drop at two Central. The Panthers are five, four and two, fifth in the Atlantic with twelve points. They lost four to nothing at Pittsburgh on Thursday, but before that beat Vancouver in overtime and Seattle seven to two. Florida is banged up, with Jacob Markstrom, Aleksander Barkov, Brad Marchand and Jonah Gadjovich all out, so Akira Schmid should start in goal, and defenseman Gustav Forsling is questionable after taking a puck to the face.

Around the league. The big rumor mill is about Winnipeg goalie Connor Hellebuyck, whose suspension for not reporting to camp is ending this week. Darren Dreger posted that a deal could be close, and Elliotte Friedman is reporting that New Jersey and Winnipeg have been working on a trade, possibly with a third team involved. Friedman also says Carolina has an offer on the table. None of that is confirmed. Another rumor: TSN's Chris Johnston reports Detroit captain Dylan Larkin wants out and has expanded his trade list to ten American teams. Montreal is not on it, though Johnston says the Canadiens have not given up hope. And Vancouver's Brock Boeser called his team's struggles embarrassing after a loss to Detroit, comments the Hockey Writers say could revive trade talk.

Injuries and discipline. Canadiens captain Nick Suzuki is out two to four weeks with an upper body injury, ending a streak of more than five hundred straight games. Oilers defenseman Mattias Ekholm is expected to miss one to two weeks after a hit from Carolina's William Carrier, and goalie Devon Levi went on injured reserve. Penguins defenseman Samuel Girard is on injured reserve, and forward Bryan Rust will be reevaluated in six weeks. Senators defenseman Thomas Chabot is out one to two weeks. On discipline, Chris Johnston reports no suspensions after the Panthers and Senators game, but Matthew Tkachuk was fined five thousand dollars for punching Dylan Cozens, and Ottawa's Nick Cousins was fined the maximum for unsportsmanlike conduct from the bench.

On to the leaderboards. In goals, Leon Draisaitl and Kirill Kaprizov are tied for the league lead with twelve. Four players are tied for third with nine: Auston Matthews, Alexis Lafreniere, Artturi Lehkonen and Kyle Connor. In assists, Connor McDavid is far out front with twenty five. Adam Fox has sixteen, Evan Bouchard has fourteen, and Nathan MacKinnon and Jack Eichel are tied with thirteen. In points, McDavid leads with thirty three, Draisaitl has twenty three, Bouchard twenty one, MacKinnon twenty, and Kaprizov and Matthews are tied at nineteen.

Finally, the standings. In the Atlantic, Boston leads with nineteen points in twelve games. In the Metropolitan, Carolina leads with twenty points. In the Central, Utah and Minnesota are tied with sixteen points each. In the Pacific, Edmonton and Vegas are tied with seventeen. Carolina is first overall with twenty points, though Edmonton leads the league by points percentage, with seventeen points in just eleven games. At the bottom, Vancouver is last with five points, and Los Angeles and Chicago are tied for second to last with six points each. Los Angeles has played nine games to Chicago's ten, so the Kings have the better points percentage.

That's your rundown. Enjoy the Sunday games, and we'll see you tomorrow.
